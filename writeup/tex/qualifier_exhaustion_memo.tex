\begin{abstract}
The investigation in \S4.2 of the application paper closed three
climatology-axis experiments on the parametric object
\((\mu_{m,h}, \sigma_{m,h})\) and reached a HOLD verdict on
de-seasonalisation removal with branch (iii) confirmed (the additive
sub-step is doing real work independent of dimension). Two engineering
candidates remain inside that family but both feed transformed series
into the same Gaussian local kernel, so neither in principle reaches
the heavy-tail cost. The question this memo answers is whether the
\(\kQ\) framework permits any method that accommodates the coupling,
and if not, why.

The exhaustion is \emph{staged}. At each stage we attempt to settle
every one of the three qualifiers --- \textbf{Register},
\textbf{Imposed Gaussianity}, \textbf{Single step} --- as
\textsc{open}, \textsc{foreclosed-at-stage}, or
\textsc{requires-experiment}. We escalate only when all three reach
a definite verdict. The three stages are: (1) strict \(\kQ\)-only;
(2) \(\kQ\) plus extensions the framework's own language admits
(e.g.\ law-agnostic local kernels); (3) any Markov-semigroup-coherent
estimator class.

The verdict shape: the framework \emph{permits} accommodation via
four design options (\textsf{S}~state, \textsf{H}~horizon,
\textsf{K}~kernel, \textsf{S+K}~combined); empirical comparison on
Ontario at \(n = 132\) days finds them all marginal-to-null under
clean preprocessing; and a Patra--Sen contamination quantification
shows \(\geq 49\%\)--\(74\%\) of per-anchor library mass is
non-Gaussian mixture mass --- large but structurally orthogonal to
the lagged-\(z\) state our levers operated on. Exogenous information,
not state-augmentation or kernel-form changes within \(z\)'s lag
span, is the empirically licensed direction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and inheritance}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{What we inherit from the paper.} The application paper's
\S2.2 defines \(\kQ\) via Rokhlin disintegration of the joint law
\(Q\) over the coarsened \(\sigma\)-algebra induced by the delay
embedding, gives the Chapman--Kolmogorov-derived Markov semigroup
\(\{\Pi_t\}\), and states the Koopman--Perron duality. The estimator
\(\Pij\) targets the one-step kernel: per anchor \(j\), a local
linear drift \(\Cj\) and a Gaussian diffusion \(\Sj\) define
\(\Pij(\cdot \mid x) = \Nrm(x\Cj,\; \Sj)\). The three qualifiers
that \S2.2 documents as the ways \(\Pij\) departs from \(\kQ\) are:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item[Register.] \(\kQ\) is a disclosure object (the conditional
  law that exists by Rokhlin); \(\Pij\) is a parametric construction
  inside that disclosure --- a specific local-linear-Gaussian
  realisation. The framework permits other realisations.
  \item[Imposed Gaussianity.] The conditional \emph{law} is fit as
  Gaussian by estimator design (WLS gives \(\Cj\), residual
  covariance gives \(\Sj\), and these together specify a Gaussian
  predictive law). \(\kQ\) itself is law-agnostic; Gaussianity is
  not a \(\kQ\)-side commitment.
  \item[Single step.] \(\Pij\) targets \(\Pi_1\) only; multi-step
  predictions are obtained by composition (\(\Pi_1^k\)), which carries
  a propagation gate (Task~\#11/12) but does not refit per horizon.
  \(\kQ\) supports the full semigroup \(\{\Pi_t\}\); the estimator
  occupies only the one-step generator.
\end{description}

\paragraph{What we inherit from results.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item Rebaseline (\textsc{claim-grade}): one-step scalar
  innovation excess kurtosis \(\approx 24\)--\(33\) across day-types
  on the intra-day-conditioned residual; tail ratio \(\approx
  2.4\)--\(3.8\). This is the heavy-tail evidence the memo is trying
  to accommodate.
  \item Error-decomposition (\textsc{method/design-grade}): on the
  validated full population (132 days via \(d_{\max}\)-augmented
  propagation), \(M_1 \approx 4.8\%\), \(M_2 \approx 2.3\%\); CIs
  overlap; neither drift- nor diffusion-deficiency dominates.
  \item \S4.2 disambiguation (\textsc{inspection-only}):
  forecast-MAPE ranking at matched dim \(\{2,4,3\}\) is seasonal
  \(3.86\% \to\) scale-only \(13.13\% \to\) no-transform \(13.57\%\).
  The additive \(\mu_{m,h}\) step alone carries
  \(\approx 9.3\) pp of the \(\approx 9.7\) pp total cost; the
  scale step \(\approx 0.4\) pp; the dimension shift
  \(\approx 1.1\) pp.
  \item \#41 (\textsc{resolved}): no prediction-error
  cross-validation localises a bandwidth on this estimator on any
  tested system; the operator-as-run is a global linear drift. Any
  Stage-2/3 lever that adds a hyperparameter is subject to this
  failure mode if naively selected by CV.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The coupling the memo must accommodate.} Strongly
non-Gaussian one-step innovation; both drift and diffusion
deficiencies materialise; the additive level step is irreducibly
entangled with forecast accuracy in the parametric \((\mu, \sigma)\)
family. A Gaussian local kernel fed a level-and-scale-stripped
embedding is fundamentally a proxy.

\paragraph{Exit condition (locked).} Each stage produces a
three-row table: \{Register, Imposed Gaussianity, Single step\}
\(\times\) \{verdict, mechanism, evidence, closing condition\}. We
escalate only when all three rows hold a definite verdict.

\section{Stage 1 --- Strict \(\kQ\)-only (no redefinition)}
\label{sec:stage1}

\paragraph{Licensing (partition declaration).} The \(\kQ\)
disclosure is law-agnostic and supports the full semigroup
\(\{\Pi_t\}\); it does not by itself commit to any specific
estimator class. To make the staging informative we declare a
\emph{partition} at Stage 1: the local estimator class stays
\emph{local-linear-Gaussian} and the disclosure register stays
unchanged; what varies is (i) the \emph{state} the realisation is
built on and (ii) the \emph{horizon} \(\Pi_t\) being targeted.
Stage 2 relaxes the estimator class explicitly. Stage 1's
foreclosures of Imposed Gaussianity are foreclosures \emph{at this
partition}, not \(\kQ\)-side commitments.

\paragraph{Register at Stage 1.} \(\kQ\) is defined via the Rokhlin
disintegration of \(Q\) over the \(\sigma\)-algebra induced by the
observation pair \((Q, F)\). The embedding \emph{defines} that
\(\sigma\)-algebra: changing it gives a different \((Q, F)\) and
therefore a different disclosure object
\(\kappa_{Q', F'} \neq \kappa_{Q, F}\). The framework permits this ---
the Rokhlin theorem applies to any well-defined observation pair ---
but the resulting estimand is a different conditional law, not a
re-reading of the same one. Adding a derivative coordinate, an
explicit phase coordinate, or an exogenous covariate is therefore a
substantive Stage-1 move: it stays inside the local-linear-Gaussian
class and inside the disclosure register but it targets
\(\kappa_{Q,\mathrm{new}}\). The \S6 conclusion of the application
paper already names this as the higher-leverage path.
\textbf{Verdict: OPEN, responsive, with estimand-change caveat.}

\paragraph{Imposed Gaussianity at Stage 1.} Under the partition, the
estimator class is held fixed at local-linear-Gaussian. Relaxing the
local kernel is by stipulation a Stage-2 move. Within Stage 1, the
only route to make a Gaussian local kernel \emph{adequate} is to
change the state so that the conditional law under the new
\((Q, F)\) coarsening is approximately Gaussian --- i.e.\ this
qualifier reduces to Register at Stage 1. \textbf{Verdict:
FORECLOSED-AT-STAGE (partition-induced); see Stage 2 for the
substantive verdict.}

\paragraph{Single step at Stage 1.} \(\Pi_h\) for \(h > 1\) is in
the disclosure register; fitting a separate local-linear-Gaussian
realisation per horizon \(h\) is permitted. The empirical question
is whether direct multi-step fitting \emph{addresses} the heavy-tail
concern --- the per-horizon residual must actually be close to
Gaussian for a Gaussian local kernel at that horizon to be adequate.
This is verifiable from the rebaselined augmented-scope cache (132-day
population, propagated \(s_k^2\)) without any new estimator work.

\section{Stage 2 --- \(\kQ\) plus named extensions}
\label{sec:stage2}

\paragraph{Licensing.} Stage 2 relaxes the partition: the estimator
class may now change. A relaxation is open if it lives inside the
law-agnostic content of \(\kQ\) --- the Rokhlin conditional
distribution is whatever \(Q\) discloses; Gaussianity was an
estimator-side imposition. Replacing the Gaussian local kernel with
a parametric heavy-tailed form (e.g.\ local Student-\(t\) with
estimated \(\nu\)) or a local non-parametric kernel is an extension
of the estimator, not of \(\kQ\). The framework permits this
directly.

\paragraph{Imposed Gaussianity at Stage 2 --- the stage's
load-bearing qualifier.} Three concrete relaxations sit inside the
stage's licensing:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \emph{Local Student-\(t\)} with anchor-level \(\nuhat_j\).
  Fit \((\Cj, \Sj)\) as now; replace the Gaussian predictive
  likelihood with Student-\(t(\nuhat_j,\; x\Cj,\; \Sj)\). The
  rebaseline kurt \(\approx 24\)--\(33\) is exactly the regime where
  a Student-\(t\) with low \(\nu\) (around \(4\)--\(5\)) is the
  canonical fit. The existing error-decomposition memo
  (\texttt{run\_error\_decomposition.py}) already uses the
  method-of-moments \(\nuhat = 6/\kappa + 4\) construction; \(M_2\)
  reports relative-loss reduction \(dB \approx 2.3\%\) (CI overlaps
  the drift counterfactual but is non-zero).
  \item \emph{Local non-parametric kernel.} Use the empirical
  distribution of local residuals as the predictive law. Maximal
  flexibility; cost: a KDE bandwidth that cannot be CV-selected
  (\#41 bars it), and predictive moments do not compose cleanly
  through the rank-1 \(J\) for multi-step propagation.
  \item \emph{Mixture locals} --- per-anchor finite Gaussian
  mixture. Framework-consistent; cost: component-count and weight
  selection, same \#41 caveat.
\end{enumerate}
Relaxation~1 has the most existing evidence, is the cheapest to
implement (drop-in likelihood swap), and propagates cleanly.
\textbf{Verdict: OPEN, responsive.} Closing condition: a local-\(t\)
estimator with non-CV \(\nu\)-selection (per-anchor
method-of-moments \(\nuhat_j\), or a global pooled \(\hat\nu\)) and
re-measurement of \(M_1/M_2\).

\paragraph{Register at Stage 2.} Estimator-class relaxation is
orthogonal to state choice; Stage 1's Register verdict is intact.
The new licensing additionally opens a \emph{combined} lever: state
augmentation + non-Gaussian local kernel. \textbf{Verdict: OPEN,
responsive (combined lever now also open).}

\paragraph{Single step at Stage 2.} Two refinements over Stage 1:
(i)~direct-h fitting with a non-Gaussian local kernel at each
horizon; (ii)~hybrid --- direct-h with local-\(t\) at short horizons
where per-step kurt is high, composition with Gaussian at long
horizons where the cached kurt-decay says Gaussian is approximately
adequate. Theoretical caveat: composition of Student-\(t\) local
kernels does not in general yield Student-\(t\) marginals; the
composed law's moment behaviour must be measured, not assumed.
\textbf{Verdict: OPEN, responsive (hybrid design now also open).}

(state augmentation; per-horizon direct fitting; local kernel-form

\section{Stage 3 --- Markov-semigroup generalisations (not load-bearing)}
\label{sec:stage3}

Stage 2 settled the primary question affirmatively, so Stage 3 is
included only for structural completeness. Dropping the \(\kQ\)
register opens additional classes (generator-based parametrisations;
latent-variable augmentations outside the original \((Q, F)\) pair;
operator-learning approaches that parametrise \(\{\Pi_t\}\)
jointly). All three qualifiers remain OPEN by inheritance from
Stages 1--2; the additional Stage-3 levers depart from the disclosure
register and incur framework cost for benefits the Stage-1/Stage-2
levers already deliver. \textbf{Not preferred over Stages 1--2.}

\paragraph{Comparison ordering.} Option K was implemented first as
the cheapest move (drop-in likelihood swap; no new pipeline, state,
or propagation), with the strongest existing evidence

kernel-form lever. S followed as the structural-lever comparator
(adding a derivative coordinate is the simplest state-augmentation).

upper-bound design; running it before S and K individual signals
were available would have repeated the \S4.2 disambiguation trap.

the existing pipeline; selection rules must be plug-in
(method-of-moments, Silverman, scaled-MAD) or structural, never

\section{Empirical landing: Option K (Student-\(t\) local kernel)}
\label{sec:option-k-result}

\paragraph{Protocol as run.} Per-day-type \(\nuhat\) via
method-of-moments (\(\nuhat = \max(4.5, 6/\kappa + 4)\); \#41-safe);
leave-one-day-out gate (fit \(\nuhat\) on \(131\) days, score on the
held-out day, rotate); all horizons via Monte-Carlo composition
(\(N_{\mathrm{MC}} = 500\)) through the augmented
\((J, \Sigma)\) propagation. Pre-flight: an MC-vs-analytic variance
sanity check confirmed the propagation convention matches the
gate-validated \texttt{propagate\_predictive\_cov} at
\(h \in \{1, 6, 12, 24\}\) within Monte-Carlo noise. The
load-bearing horizon \(h = 1\) used closed-form Gaussian and
Student-\(t\) densities.

\paragraph{Pre-registered reading conventions.} The relative-loss
bar \(\tau_{\min} = 0.01\) is read two ways: \emph{lenient} (point
estimate \(> \tau_{\min}\) and CI excludes zero --- the original
\texttt{run\_error\_decomposition.py} pre-registration);
\emph{strict} (CI lower bound \(> \tau_{\min}\)). Both readings
appear below; the lenient bar is the operative pre-reg.

\paragraph{Pooled headline.}

  & \(0.0238\) & \([0.0071,\ 0.0414]\) & lenient PASS / strict fail \\

The LOO + per-day-type + closed-form-at-\(h{=}1\) tightening did
not materially change the magnitude from the original \(M_2\):
\(\approx 2.4\%\) vs.\ \(\approx 2.3\%\).

\paragraph{Per-horizon detail.} Under the strict per-horizon
interpretation, only \(h = 3\) and \(h = 4\) cleanly clear
\(\tau_{\min}\); under the lenient bar, \(h \in \{3, 4, 8, 9\}\)
add that their CIs exclude zero with point estimate above
\(\tau_{\min}\). Routine-day per-day win-rate is \(56.7\%\) on
average across horizons --- at \(n = 132\) that is \(\approx 1.5\)
standard errors above the \(50\%\) null and does not on its own
constitute a statistical signal.

\paragraph{Mid-horizon signal.} Per-horizon day-bootstrap excluding
first-of-month delivery days (see \S\ref{sec:option-k-seam-audit}
for why exclusion is the operative reading): \(h = 3\) PASS strict
(CI \([+0.16,\ +25.65]\); raw \(\Delta\mathrm{LL} = +0.03\) nats);
\(h = 4\) PASS strict (\(dB = 0.15\); CI \([+0.05,\ +0.32]\));
\(h = 8, 9\) PASS lenient (\(dB \approx 0.02\); CI cleanly above
zero). Pooled excl-first-of-month \(dB = 0.018\) (CI
\([0.005,\ 0.031]\)); excluding \(h = 1\) additionally leaves the
pooled signal at \(dB = 0.018\) (CI \([0.004,\ 0.031]\)) --- the
pooled signal does not depend on the \(h = 1\) contribution. The
improvement is concentrated where the Stage-1 kurt-decay analysis
predicted the kernel-form gap to live: mid-horizons (\(h = 3\)--\(9\))
where compositional kurt is dropping but not yet near zero.

\paragraph{Retraction --- the apparent \(h = 1\) catastrophe channel
was a pipeline artefact.} This section originally claimed a prominent
\(h = 1\) catastrophic-event channel, attributing the pooled signal
to one day (\(2021\text{-}12\text{-}01\); a \(\approx 7\sigma\) event
under the Gaussian one-step law; single-day Student-\(t\) advantage
\(16.2\) nats). The mechanism is in fact a separable pipeline issue
(a month-boundary climatology lookup discontinuity, documented in
\S\ref{sec:option-k-seam-audit}), not an intrinsic heavy-tail
property of the conditional law. Two further facts settle the
retraction: (a)~the \(h = 1\) signal does not survive seam
exclusion (pooled \(h = 1\) \(\Delta\mathrm{LL}\) drops from
\(+0.12\) to \(-0.02\); both CIs straddle zero --- the catastrophe-
channel claim was not statistically supported \emph{even on the
contaminated data}); (b)~the pooled K verdict is robust (\(dB\) drops
from \(0.024\) to \(0.018\) under seam exclusion, CI excludes zero
in both cases). What Student-\(t\) actually provides is a modest
mid-horizon improvement, not h=1 catastrophe insurance.

\paragraph{Closing-condition reading.} The Stage-2 closing condition
asked for re-measured \(M_1/M_2\) under the new likelihood, clearing
\(\tau_{\min}\). With seam exclusion as the operative measurement:
under the lenient bar, pooled excl-first-of-month \(dB = 1.8\%\)
clears --- condition \textbf{met}; under the strict bar, CI lower
bound is \(0.5\%\), below the \(1\%\) bar --- not met. The original
pre-registration used the lenient reading. \textbf{Verdict on
Option K: PASS (lenient pre-reg)}, with modest effect size
(\(\approx 1.8\%\) relative log-loss reduction) localised at
mid-horizons.

\section{Pipeline finding: the month-boundary climatology seam}
\label{sec:option-k-seam-audit}

\paragraph{Discovery.} Extending the dev window from \(n = 132\) to
\(n = 365\) days surfaced a structural issue in the deseasonalisation
pipeline. At \(n = 365\) the pooled \(h = 1\) standardised-residual
kurt rises to \(19.1\); excluding the \(12\) first-of-month
delivery days (\(3.3\%\) of the sample) drops it to \(0.46\).
Mechanism, verified on \(2022\text{-}04\text{-}01\): raw demand
fell normally from \(13\,808\) MW at \(23\):\(00\) on
\(2022\text{-}03\text{-}31\) to \(13\,534\) MW at \(00\):\(00\) on
\(2022\text{-}04\text{-}01\) (an unremarkable early-morning trough),
but the \(z\)-score jumped from \(-0.87\) to \(+0.48\) because
\(z\) at \(23\):\(00\) uses
\((\mu_{\mathrm{Mar},23}, \sigma_{\mathrm{Mar},23})\) and \(z\)
at \(00\):\(00\) uses \((\mu_{\mathrm{Apr},0}, \sigma_{\mathrm{Apr},0})\).
The local-linear model treats \(z\) as continuous and has no
machinery for this discontinuity; the production estimator handles
an analogous day-anchor seam at hour \(7\)
(\texttt{day\_anchor\_hour} masks transitions whose target lands on
that hour) but has no analogous month-boundary mask.

\section{Empirical landing: Option S (state augmentation)}
\label{sec:option-s-result}

\paragraph{Protocol as run.} A first-pass design used a derivative
coordinate \(\dot z_t = z_t - z_{t-1}\) added to the lag state, but
under a linear WLS fit, the derivative lives in the linear span of
\((z_t, z_{t-1})\) and augmenting with it is mathematically
equivalent to enriching the lag depth (no discriminating power for
Register). The revised design uses
\(\phi_t = \tanh\!\bigl((z_t - z_{t-1}) / s_{\mathrm{dt}}\bigr)\)
where \(s_{\mathrm{dt}}\) is the dev-set median \(|\dot z|\) per
day-type --- a nonlinear function of past \(z\) that the linear WLS
fit cannot recover from raw lags. A confound-control side arm
(\textsf{lag}) uses pure delay coordinates at the same augmented
dimension \(d + 1\) to check whether any kurt change is specifically
attributable to the nonlinear coordinate or merely to a larger state.
Scope: \(h = 1\) (the load-bearing horizon for the kernel-form
question); multi-step propagation under nonlinear \(\phi_{t+1}\)
would require EKF-style linearisation and is deferred.

\paragraph{Residual kurt by arm.}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r}
\toprule
arm & pooled & weekday (\(n{\approx}95\)) & sat/sun (\(n{=}19\), qual.) \\
\midrule
baseline (cache, \(d\) per elbow) & \(8.60\) & \(10.24\) & \(1.50\) / \(2.72\) \\
\textsf{phi} (lag + \(\phi_t\), \(d+1\)) & \(9.77\) & \(8.55\) & \(3.29\) / \(8.59\) \\
\textsf{lag} (\(d + 1\) lags, no \(\phi\)) & \(10.13\) & \(8.65\) & \(3.70\) / \(9.39\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Pooled-kurt CIs are wide (\(\approx [1, 15]\)) and overlap across
arms. Weekday point estimates suggest a small (\(\approx 1.5\)-unit)
reduction under both \textsf{phi} and \textsf{lag}, but bootstrap
noise is large relative to the difference. \textsf{phi} and
\textsf{lag} agree to within \(0.5\) units everywhere --- the
nonlinear \(\phi\) coordinate is not contributing distinguishable
work beyond what bigger state alone delivers.

\textbf{Verdict on the kurt gate: S-iii.} The residual kurt is
statistically unchanged by either augmentation. The heavy-tail
property of the per-step conditional law is not removable by state
augmentation within this family at this measurement resolution.

\paragraph{Retraction --- the \(dB(K | S)\) drop was seam-driven.}
A first reading observed that pooled \(dB(K)\) under the
\textsf{phi}-augmented state dropped \(16\times\) (\(4.56\%\)
baseline \(\to 0.28\%\) phi), and proposed a mechanism: the augmented
WLS produces \(\sigma_{00} \approx 2\times\) baseline, which deflates
the catastrophe day's \(z_{\mathrm{std}}\) from \(-7.4\) to
\(-5.3\). Both observations were factually correct; the seam audit
changes their interpretation:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r}
\toprule
arm & all-days \(dB\) & excl-first-of-month \(dB\) & excl-first \(dB\) CI \\
\midrule
baseline    & \(+4.56\)  & \(-0.008\) & \([-0.066, +0.045]\) \\
\textsf{phi} & \(+0.28\) & \(+0.028\) & \([-0.003, +0.052]\) \\
\textsf{lag} & \(+0.31\) & \(+0.026\) & \([-0.009, +0.051]\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The \(16\times\) drop \emph{dissolves} under seam exclusion. On
routine days, all three arms have \(dB \in [-0.008, +0.028]\) with
CIs straddling zero. The \(\sigma_{00}\) median is \(0.0154\) for
baseline and \(\approx 0.0287\) for both augmented arms ---
\emph{uniform} across all anchors, not concentrated at seam-near
ones. The original mechanistic story
(``\(\sigma\)-inflation tames catastrophe events'') is therefore
\textbf{retracted}: the \(\sigma\)-doubling is a global property of
the higher-dim WLS fit, not a seam-responsive property, and its
observable consequence on \(dB\) was specifically the deflation of
seam-event extremes.

\paragraph{Open mechanism question.} The \(\sigma_{00}\) doubling
under state augmentation is itself unexplained. Three candidate
mechanisms are plausible: (i)~curse-of-dimensionality on the
local-residual variance estimate (sparser local neighbourhood gives
higher residual variance); (ii)~a genuinely worse-on-average WLS fit
under augmented state; (iii)~LOO-CV bandwidth selection responding
differently to richer state. Distinguishing them requires a designed
experiment outside this memo's scope; flagged for follow-up.

\section{Contamination quantification (Patra--Sen)}
\label{sec:patra-sen}

\paragraph{Question.} The qualifier-exhaustion programme established
that the framework \emph{permits} levers against the heavy-tailed
conditional law and that the Ontario empirical landings on those
levers were marginal or null. How much of the per-anchor
library-residual mass is genuinely non-Gaussian mixture mass,
distinct from the predictive-Gaussian null \(\mathcal{N}(0,1)\)?
Patra \& Sen (2016)\footnote{Patra, R.\ K.,
and B.\ Sen, ``Estimation of a two-component mixture model with
applications to multiple testing,'' \emph{Journal of the Royal
Statistical Society Series B} 78(4):869--893 (2016); arXiv:1204.5488.}
give a published consistent estimator \(\hat\alpha_0\) of the mixing
proportion in \(F = \alpha F_s + (1-\alpha) F_b\) with \(F_b\) fully
specified, and a distribution-free finite-sample lower confidence
bound \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%}\) (their Theorem~5) satisfying
\(\mathbb{P}(\alpha_0 \geq \hat\alpha_L^{95\%}) \geq 0.95\) and
\(\mathbb{P}(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} = 0 \mid \alpha_0 = 0) \geq 0.95\)
(an exact homogeneity test at the null). The Patra--Sen
identifiability result (their Lemma~4) implies that for
contamination sharing support with \(F_b\) (which heavy-tailed
contamination does), \(\hat\alpha_0\) is a \emph{lower bound} on
the true mixing fraction.

\paragraph{Protocol.} For each (day-type \(\times\) preprocessing),
draw 30 random anchors from the pre-cutoff intra-day library; at
each anchor, take the scalar library residual
\(r_0 = z_{t+1} - x_t \cdot C_j[:,0]\) returned by the production-path
\texttt{local\_drift\_and\_diffusion}, standardise by
\(\sqrt{\Sigma_j[0,0]}\); subsample to a cap of \(2{,}000\) per
anchor; pool to \(n_{\mathrm{pooled}} = 60{,}000\) per cell. Apply
Patra--Sen with \(F_b = \Phi\) at the published tuning
(\(c_n = 0.1 \log\log n\) for the point estimate; \(c_n = 0.6792\)
for the \(95\%\) lower CI, Anderson--Darling quantile).

\paragraph{Implementation provenance.} The Python PAVA-based
estimator (\texttt{scratch/patra\_sen.py}) was verified
against the paper's Figure~1 synthetic settings
(\(\hat\alpha\) within \(\sim 0.02\) of truth at \(\alpha = 0.10\),
\(n = 5000\)); the null setting \(\alpha = 0\) gave
\(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} = 0.002\) (exact lower-bound property of
Theorem~5 confirmed).

\paragraph{Result.}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r r r}
\toprule
condition & \(d\) & pooled kurt & \(\hat\alpha\) & \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%}\) & anchor-seed range \\
\midrule
\multicolumn{6}{l}{\textbf{Month-hour preprocessing}} \\
weekday  & 2 & \(40.00\) & \(0.552\) & \(0.490\) & \([0.490, 0.505]\) \\
saturday & 2 & \(27.84\) & \(0.725\) & \(0.637\) & \([0.605, 0.637]\) \\
sunday   & 4 & \(27.98\) & \(0.803\) & \(0.742\) & \([0.738, 0.747]\) \\

weekday  & 3 & \(22.01\) & \(0.583\) & \(0.512\) & \([0.508, 0.520]\) \\
saturday & 3 & \(20.46\) & \(0.597\) & \(0.520\) & \([0.518, 0.535]\) \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Every cell rejects the homogeneity null
(\(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} > 0\)) decisively; the rebaseline-grade
heavy-tail finding is now quantified as a \emph{lower bound} on the
mixing fraction \(\alpha_0\), spanning \(0.49\) to \(0.74\) across
the six conditions.

\paragraph{Nested stability checks the result survives.} The
\(0.49\)--\(0.74\) lower-bound range is robust under each of the
following independent checks:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \emph{Synthetic null}: VAR(1) with Gaussian innovations
  through the identical production estimator
  (\texttt{processing/innovations/validation/synthetic}) gives
  \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} = 0.002\). The local-WLS-fit-and-pool
  protocol does not produce false positives.
  \item \emph{Single heavy-tailed AR(1) rule-out}: AR(1) with
  Student-\(t(\nu)\) innovations at population-kurt-matched
  \(\nu \in \{4.27, 4.10, 4.05, 4.02, 4.01\}\) gives
  \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} \in [0.20, 0.24]\). Ontario
  \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%}\) is \(2.0\)--\(3.5\times\) the maximum any
  single AR(1)+\(t\) configuration produces --- Ontario residuals
  are not a single heavy-tailed AR(1).
  \item \emph{Moment-vs-CDF robustness}: at sample-kurt-matched
  \(\nu = 4.01\) across five RNG seeds,
  \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} \in [0.218, 0.262]\). Sample kurts fluctuate
  \(6\) to \(22\) across seeds at this \(\nu\) (the fourth moment
  doesn't exist for \(\nu \le 4\)), but \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%}\) is
  rock-stable across the same seeds --- Patra--Sen's CDF-based
  functional is robust where moment statistics are not.
  \item \emph{Anchor-seed stability across all six cells}: spread
  \(< 0.04\) on every cell (table above, last column).
  \item \emph{Homogeneity rejection across cells}: every cell
  passes \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} > 0\) decisively.
  \item \emph{Preprocessing-independence}: Fourier preprocessing
  drops pooled kurt substantially (40\(\to\)22 weekday;
  28\(\to\)20 saturday) but \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%}\) shifts much less
  (weekday 0.49\(\to\)0.51; saturday 0.64\(\to\)0.52; sunday
  unchanged at 0.74). The seam affected tail shape (kurt); the
  underlying mixture structure is preprocessing-independent.
  \item \emph{Identifiability direction (Lemma~4)}: for contamination
  sharing support with \(F_b\), \(\hat\alpha_0\) estimates
  \(\alpha_0 = \alpha \cdot \{1 - \operatorname{ess\,inf}(f_s/f_b)\}
  \leq \alpha\). The headline \(0.49\)--\(0.74\) therefore
  \emph{underestimates} the true mixing fraction. Honest framing:
  ``Patra--Sen's \(\alpha_0\) (a lower bound on the true mixing
  fraction) is \(0.49\)--\(0.74\) in Ontario.''
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Reading.} The non-Gaussian library-residual mass is
genuinely substantial: \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} \geq 0.49\) under every
condition, with the bound conservatively underestimating the true
\(\alpha\). Combined with the qualifier-exhaustion programme's
negative empirical lever-comparison findings, this reframes the
practical conclusion: the contamination is large in mass but
\emph{structurally orthogonal} to the lagged-\(z\) state our levers
operated on. Exogenous information (temperature, calendar event
flags, weather regime indicators), not state-augmentation or
kernel-form changes within \(z\)'s lag span, is the empirically
licensed direction.

\section{Cross-layer synthesis and integration decision}
\label{sec:integration}

\paragraph{Three layers, kept distinct.} The work in this memo
produces findings at three different levels of the question
``does the \(\kQ\) framework permit a method that accommodates the
heavy-tail / forecast-accuracy coupling?''. Conflating them would
misread either the framework verdict or the empirical landing.

\paragraph{Layer 1 --- programme: the framework verdict is
constructive.} The qualifier-exhaustion analysis
(\S\S\,\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:framework-synthesis}) identifies
four framework-consistent design options
(\textsf{S, H, K, S+K}) under Stages 1 and 2; all are licensed by
\(\kQ\)'s law-agnostic content. The premise that motivated the
exhaustion --- a strongly heavy-tailed conditional law (rebaseline
kurt \(24\)--\(33\)) --- is production-path-validated and seam-
independent (\S\,\ref{sec:option-k-seam-audit}). \emph{The framework
permits accommodation; no foreclosure is required.}

\paragraph{Layer 2 --- empirical: per-arm landings on the
\(n = 132\) dev set, seam exclusion as primary.}

\paragraph{Layer 3 --- contamination: large mass, orthogonal to
\(z\)-state.} The Patra--Sen quantification
(\S\,\ref{sec:patra-sen}) places at least \(49\%\)--\(74\%\) of
per-anchor library mass in the non-Gaussian mixture component, with
that bound conservatively underestimating the true fraction
(Lemma~4). The contamination is intrinsic to \(\kappa_Q\) (not
preprocessing-induced; seven nested stability checks survive). The
only consistent reading of large contamination mass alongside
null empirical levers is that the contamination is structurally
orthogonal to the lagged-\(z\) state on which \textsf{S}, \textsf{K},
\textsf{S+K}, and \textsf{H} all operate.

\paragraph{Pipeline finding documented as limitation.} The
month-boundary climatology lookup discontinuity
(\S\,\ref{sec:option-k-seam-audit}) is a structural issue in the
deseasonalisation pipeline, separable from the framework verdict
but observably contaminating forecast-cache evidence at
\(\sim 12\) events per year. Production fix: a target-side mask
analogous to \texttt{day\_anchor\_hour}. Out of scope here;
documented as a known limitation for the application paper.

\paragraph{What this leaves unresolved.} Two questions remain
flagged for future work: (a)~the \(\sigma_{00}\) doubling under
state augmentation
(\S\,\ref{sec:option-s-result}, three candidate mechanisms); and
(b)~whether at \(n \gg 132\), with the seam fixed, the cross-arm
differences become statistically resolvable --- the per-day-type
drift SEs suggest even \(n = 365\) is unlikely to resolve them
cleanly without pipeline-level changes.

\paragraph{Integration into the application paper.} A short paragraph
in \S4.3 carries the verdict at all three layers:

\emph{Framework.} The \(\kappa_Q\) programme permits kernel-form,
state-augmentation, and per-horizon relaxations; its law-agnostic
content supports four design options
(\textsf{K}, \textsf{S}, \textsf{H}, \textsf{S+K}). Rebaseline-grade
evidence (per-anchor library kurt \(24\)--\(33\)) of heavy-tailedness
motivates the search.

\emph{Empirical contamination magnitude.} At least \(49\%\)--\(74\%\)
of per-anchor library mass is non-Gaussian mixture mass (Patra--Sen
\(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%}\), conservative lower bound by Lemma~4). The
contamination is \emph{not} a small heavy-tail perturbation of a
near-Gaussian conditional law.

\emph{Empirical activation of the framework levers.} Under clean
(Fourier) preprocessing, none of \textsf{K}, \textsf{S},
\textsf{S+K}, \textsf{H} delivers a statistically clean
forecast-accuracy improvement on routine-day data. Combined with the
contamination quantification, the only consistent reading is that
the contamination is structurally orthogonal to the lagged-\(z\)
state on which all four levers operate. \emph{Exogenous information}
(temperature, weather regime, calendar event flags), not
state-augmentation or kernel-form changes within the past-\(z\)
information set, is the empirically licensed direction.

The month-boundary seam is documented as a separable preprocessing
limitation and Fourier preprocessing is adopted as the cleaner
default. The sibling-memo arrangement (this document) is the right
place for the detailed exhaustion analysis; the application paper's
\S4.3 inherits the verdict statement above.
