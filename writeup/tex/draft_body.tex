\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

This paper frames short-horizon Ontario electricity-demand forecasting
as a question about the \emph{conditional law of a reconstructed
dynamical state}: given a delay embedding of recent demand, what is
the distribution of the next state? A local-Gaussian
conditional-kernel estimator of that law is instantiated on IESO data
and evaluated as a \emph{prediction experiment} --- with an
out-of-sample backtest, a registered forward experiment, and an
accounting of comparability against IESO's own published forecasts.

The estimator targets the conditional-regularity kernel \(\kQ\)
determined by the data measure via Rokhlin disintegration
(Section~\ref{sec:method}).

\section{Theory and method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Construction}
\label{sec:construction}

\paragraph{Embedding and library.}
Let \(s_t\) be a scalar observable (here, de-seasonalised hourly
demand; Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) sampled on a regular grid
\(t \in \mathbb{Z}\). The delay embedding into \(\Rb^{d}\) is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:embed}
x_t \;=\; (s_t,\; s_{t-1},\; \ldots,\; s_{t-(d-1)}) \,.
\end{equation}
Per day-type \(\mathrm{dt} \in \{\mathrm{weekday},
\mathrm{saturday}, \mathrm{sunday}\}\) the embedding dimension
\(d = d(\mathrm{dt})\) is selected on pre-cutoff library data by the
elbow rule on the one-step in-sample prediction-skill curve
\(\rho \mapsto d\), with the \emph{day-type seam} excluded --- here
defined as the 07:00 hour at which the active day-type changes; a
one-step pair \((x_t, x_{t+1})\) whose target time \(t+1\) falls on
that hour crosses to a different day-type's library and is dropped
from the fit (see Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}). The pre-cutoff library
is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:library}
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \{\,(x_t, x_{t+1}) : t \le t_{\mathrm{cut}},\;
t \text{ on the day-type } \mathrm{dt},\;
t+1 \notin \text{day-type seam}\,\} \,,
\end{equation}
with \(t_{\mathrm{cut}}\) the model-specification cutoff.

\paragraph{Local linear fit.}
For a query state \(x \in \Rb^{d}\), form the kernel weights
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weights}
w_i(x;\theta) \;=\; (2\pi)^{-d/2}\,\theta^{-d}\,
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert x_i - x \rVert^{2}}{2\theta^{2}}\right),
\qquad (x_i, y_i) \in \mathcal{L} \,,
\end{equation}
with bandwidth \(\theta > 0\) and \(y_i \equiv x_{t_i + 1}\). The
weighted least-squares local linear one-step map
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:drift}
x_{\text{next}} \;\approx\; x \cdot \Cj \qquad
\text{(row convention; \(j\) indexes the query state)}
\end{equation}
solves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wls}
\Cj \;=\; \arg\min_{C \in \Rb^{d \times d}}
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} w_i(x;\theta) \, \lVert y_i - x_i C \rVert^{2}
\;=\; \bigl(X^{\top} W X\bigr)^{-1} X^{\top} W Y \,,
\end{equation}
where \(X, Y\) stack the library pairs and
\(W = \operatorname{diag}(w_i)\). The plain (unweighted)
residual covariance defines the diffusion,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:diff}
\Sj \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} \bigl(r_i - \bar r\bigr)
\bigl(r_i - \bar r\bigr)^{\!\top},
\qquad r_i := y_i - x_i \Cj,
\quad \bar r := \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}\sum_i r_i \,.
\end{equation}
The pair defines the state-dependent Gaussian Markov kernel
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kernel}
\Pij(x, \cdot) \;=\; \Nrm\!\bigl(x \Cj,\; \Sj\bigr) \,.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Why the plain (unweighted) covariance.}
The kernel-weighted residual covariance
\(\Sj^{w} = \sum_i w_i\,(r_i - \bar r)(r_i - \bar r)^{\!\top}
/ \sum_i w_i\) collapses toward \(\approx w^{2}\!\cdot Q\) for the
normalised Gaussian kernel of Eq.~\ref{eq:weights}; the plain
covariance is the unbiased local second moment, free of that
collapse.

\paragraph{Bandwidth: locality of \(\Cj\).}
Can the bandwidth \(\theta\) be chosen by data so that \(\Cj\) is
local? Two candidate rules were investigated. The
Goldenshluger--Lepski
criterion in the supporting library predicts an interior optimum that
balances bias against variance; for the normalised Gaussian
kernel of Eq.~\ref{eq:weights} the GL score is monotone in \(\theta\)
with no interior optimum, so any grid returns its own
ceiling --- the GL prediction fails. True leave-one-out
cross-validation on one-step squared error,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loocv}
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LOO}}(\theta)
\;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}}
\bigl\lVert y_i - x_i \Cj^{(-i)}(\theta) \bigr\rVert^{2},
\end{equation}
where \(\Cj^{(-i)}(\theta)\) is the local fit at \(x_i\) computed
\emph{without} the point \((x_i, y_i)\), predicts a statistically
separated interior minimum at the data's true bandwidth scale. To
test this, \(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LOO}}\) was evaluated across the
anchor population under a bootstrap-SE procedure (resampling library
indices with replacement, \(B = 10^{3}\) replicates), and the
grid-argmin compared with the rest of the grid. The argmin is
\emph{not} statistically separated from any other grid point: the
LOO prediction fails as well.

This could be a peculiarity of Ontario data. To distinguish that from
a property of the criterion, the same procedure was run on three
independent controlled systems (a linear VAR(1), the logistic map,
and a regime-switching AR) and at every multi-step horizon
\(h \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 24\}\); non-discrimination reproduces in
all cases. The non-discrimination is a property of the criterion,
not of Ontario data. \(\Cj\) is effectively a single global linear
operator at the \(d = 2\) z-score-lag embedding.

With \(\Cj\) effectively global and linear, the non-Dirac content of
the predictive law is carried by \(\Sj\) and its departure from
Gaussianity (Section~\ref{sec:caveat}). The available lever is the
\emph{state representation} (richer embedding, explicit
phase/derivative coordinate), not the bandwidth rule.

\paragraph{The conditional law \(\kQ\) the estimator targets.}
On a probability space \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) with two
observations \(Q, F\), the Rokhlin disintegration
theorem~\cite{Rokhlin1952,Bogachev2007} produces a Markov kernel
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kappaQ}
\kQ(q, \cdot) \;=\; P(F \in \cdot \mid Q = q),
\end{equation}
determined by the measure --- not posited as an additional dynamical
law. Under temporal indexing of the observations, finite-dimensional
distributions are coherent, so the kernels compose: the
Chapman--Kolmogorov equation is a consequence, not a hypothesis, and
yields a Markov semigroup \(\{\Pi_t\}\)~\cite{Eisner2015}. The
companion Koopman--Perron duality
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:KP}
\int K_t\, g\, \dd\mu \;=\; \int g\, \dd(P_t^{*}\mu)
\end{equation}
relates the Koopman operator \(K_t g = g \circ T_t\) (when the
dynamics is deterministic, recovering the classical Koopman operator
when the kernels are Dirac~\cite{LasotaMackey1994}) to its
Perron--Frobenius adjoint \(P_t^{*}\) on densities. Three layers are
useful to keep notationally distinct: \(\kQ\) (the disintegration
kernel; a conditional law), \(\{\Pi_t\}\) (the time-indexed Markov
semigroup), and \(K_t\) (the operator-layer object).

\paragraph{The estimator's relationship to \(\kQ\).}
The object Section~\ref{sec:construction} constructs is a
finite-sample, parametric, single-step, Gaussian-form estimator of
\(\kQ\) at the delay-embedding layer, from which the operator-layer
objects \(K_{\Delta} / P_{\Delta}^{*}\) are recoverable in the
non-Dirac regime (\(\Sj \neq 0\)).
The \(\kQ\) correspondence holds at the embedding layer; \emph{locality}
of that estimator is not empirically operative on Ontario
(Section~\ref{sec:construction}).
\(\Cj\) is a linearisation of the conditional-mean factor (intended
local, effectively global here); \(\Sj\) is the second moment of
\(\kQ\)'s Gaussian projection; \(\Sj \to 0\) reproduces the
classical-Koopman (deterministic) collapse. Three qualifiers bound
this correspondence:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Register.} \(\kQ\) is determined by the measure; this
  work \emph{constructs} a Gaussian proxy and fits it from finite
  data --- ``estimator of,'' not ``instance of.''

  \item \textbf{Imposed Gaussianity.} \(\Pij = \Nrm(x \Cj, \Sj)\)
  coincides with \(\kQ\) only where the local conditional law is
  itself Gaussian. Section~\ref{sec:caveat} quantifies the gap as
  real but below the pre-registered actionability bar.

  \item \textbf{Single step.} \(\Pij\) is the single-step slice
  \(\Pidelta\); the semigroup \(\{\Pi_t\}\) is neither composed nor
  verified here. (A multi-step predictive-variance propagation via a
  shift-aware state Jacobian is built and gate-validated as the
  instrument for Section~\ref{sec:caveat}'s decomposition; it is not
  the semigroup.)
\end{enumerate}

\section{Structural caveat: a mis-centred conditional mean and a
Gaussian-proxy diffusion}
\label{sec:caveat}

The two estimator factors \(\Cj\) and \(\Sj\) each carry a quantified
limitation. A pre-registered decomposition on the 2021--22
development-set benchmark, with predictive variance propagated over
the full delivery-day population, finds \textbf{neither effect
clears the materiality bar}.

\paragraph{\(\Sj\) is rank-1 by construction.} For a single-variable
delay embedding, coordinates \(2, \ldots, d\) of the one-step image
are deterministic shifts of the input coordinates
(\(Y_{[:,\,1:]} = X_{[:,\,:-1]}\) exactly). The only stochastic
content of the one-step map is the scalar coordinate-0 innovation;
therefore \(\Sj\) is rank-1 by construction, independent of data ---
the multivariate diffusion is structurally a single scalar innovation
variance embedded in \(\Rb^{d \times d}\).

\paragraph{That scalar innovation is non-Gaussian.} The
non-Gaussianity question is therefore \emph{univariate}. The
estimator, validated against synthetic ground truth
(Section~\ref{sec:synth}), gives a heavy-tailed one-step innovation.

\paragraph{The conditional mean is phase-mis-centred.}
Is the conditional-mean factor faithful, leaving only the
non-Gaussianity deficit? The embedding gives the local linear map no
phase
information --- it cannot tell a state rising through a demand level
from one falling through it. The simpler reading is that this
matters little (real load dynamics are nearly time-reversible at
the relevant lag); the alternative is that the map centres between
rising and falling regimes and thereby carries a structured residual
bias. Under the simpler reading, the signed \(z\)-space error
should be uncorrelated with the demand derivative \(\dd z/\dd t\);
under the alternative, it should be correlated and monotone across
phase. The measured correlation is \(\mathrm{corr} = -0.246\)
(bootstrap 95\%
CI \([-0.283,\, -0.207]\), \(n = 3167\)), monotone across
rising/falling phase. The simpler reading fails: \(\Pij\)'s location,
not only its shape, departs from \(\kQ\).

\paragraph{Decomposition.}
\label{sec:caveat-decomposition}
A pre-registered diagnostic on the 2021--22 development-set
benchmark (exploratory) bounds the recoverable loss of each factor
by oracle counterfactuals on the multi-step forecast
log-loss \(\Lbar\), over all delivery days, with predictive variance
propagated across the embedding-dimension change.
The recoverable-loss factors are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:DA-DB}
\DA \;:=\; \frac{\Lbar - \Lbar^{(A)}}{\Lbar},
\qquad
\DB \;:=\; \frac{\Lbar - \Lbar^{(B)}}{\Lbar},
\end{equation}
where \(\Lbar^{(A)}\) is the development-set log-loss under the
oracle that removes the phase-conditional mean bias, and
\(\Lbar^{(B)}\) is the same loss under the oracle that replaces the
Gaussian innovation law with a Student-\(t\) fitted on the residuals
(shape \(\nuhat\)).
With \(\taumin = 0.01\) the pre-registered materiality threshold,
the diagnostic yields the verdicts in
Table~\ref{tab:verdict}: on the full delivery-day population
\textbf{neither lower bound clears \(\taumin\), and the \(\DB\)
interval includes zero}; on the single-dimension subpopulation
(used here as a robustness comparison) both cleared \(\taumin\) but
the CIs overlapped. The full-population verdict is therefore the
catch-all ``neither dominates'' --- not because the effects are real
and tied, but because on the complete propagated data neither is
established at all.

\paragraph{Propagation softens the apparent heavy tail at multi-step.}
\label{sec:caveat-propagation}
The decomposition operates on standardised residuals of the
multi-step forecast under propagation-aware variance. On the same
data this gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kurt-attenuation}
\nuhat \;:\; 5.0 \to 6.5,
\qquad
\mathrm{kurt}_{\mathrm{exc}} \;:\; 6.1 \to 2.4 \,,
\end{equation}
a roughly four-fold attenuation. Restricting to the single-dimension
subpopulation (\(\approx 58\%\) of days) yields the same qualitative
verdict.

\paragraph{The one-step conditional law remains heavy-tailed.} The
propagation softening is a property of the multi-step forecast
object, not of the underlying conditional law. The rebaseline
\(\mathrm{kurt}_{\mathrm{exc}} \approx 24\)--\(33\) is measured at
the per-anchor library-residual level (one-step, locally, no
propagation involved), and is preprocessing-independent at that
level. The framework-level analysis of available relaxations
(Section~\ref{sec:qualifier-verdict}) quantifies the mass of this
one-step departure directly via a Patra--Sen consistent contamination
estimator and links the empirical lever-comparison findings to it.

\section{Application and results}
\label{sec:application}

\subsection{Ontario pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

The instantiation is a configuration-driven pipeline; all paths and
parameters resolve from a single \texttt{config/pipeline.yaml}
relative to the project root (no working-directory dependence).
Stages: \textbf{acquire} IESO public \texttt{PUB\_Demand};
\textbf{de-seasonalise} (Section~\ref{sec:zscore}) hourly demand to a
stationary \(z\)-score; \textbf{day-type tag}
\(\{\mathrm{weekday}, \mathrm{saturday}, \mathrm{sunday}\}\) using a
configurable 07:00 day-anchor offset (transitions across the day-type
seam are excluded as in Section~\ref{sec:construction} --- without
this \(\Sj\) is catastrophically ill-conditioned, so the estimand is
effectively forced to be \emph{intra-day}); \textbf{embedding
dimension} selected per day-type by an elbow rule on the
prediction-skill-vs-dimension curve; \textbf{per-anchor fit} of the
Section~\ref{sec:method} estimator.

\subsection{The \(z\)-score transform is a framework coupling, not a
preprocessing detail}
\label{sec:zscore}

De-seasonalisation is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:zscore}
z \;=\; \frac{D - \mu_{m,h}}{\sigma_{m,h}},
\end{equation}
with \(\mu, \sigma\) a month \(\times\) hour-of-day climatology
estimated strictly from pre-cutoff data. This single climatology
object is woven through \emph{all four} estimator layers: the embedded
state, the drift fit, the diffusion (\(\Sj\) is the covariance of
\(z\)-residuals), and the demand-space round-trip. It is therefore an
implicit seasonal model coupled into \(\kQ\), not a benign
preprocessing step.

Is the coupling \emph{benign} (an invertible change of coordinates,
the dynamics unaffected) or \emph{leaky} (an implicit seasonal model
whose error structure is folded into the dynamics)? Under the
benign reading, the error structure should not vary with the
climatology's seasonal resolution. To test this, the pipeline was
re-run under climatology specs of varying resolution
(monthly $\times$ hour-of-day, monthly only, hour-of-day only, and
the no-transform extreme), and the per-axis error structure measured
in demand space.

On the location axis (phase/asymmetry structure), the error was
\textbf{invariant} across all specs --- the benign prediction is met
exactly, and the conditional-mean structure is demand dynamics
rather than a transform artefact. On the heavy-tail axis, however, the error structure
\emph{did} move with seasonal resolution --- the benign prediction
fails, and the coupling acts there as a partially leaky seasonal
model. This is Section~\ref{sec:caveat}'s heavy-tail caveat showing
up at the coordinate layer, not a separate defect.
\todo[inline]{Development-set investigation; its role here is
methodological --- establishing the coordinate is sound before any
subsequent re-specification.}

The follow-up question --- whether the element delivering the
location benefit is the element driving the tail leak --- spawns
three sub-questions: under what assumptions does intrinsic coupling
follow from the joint observation of location invariance and tail
movement; which sub-step of de-seasonalisation delivers the location
benefit; and whether a different family of preprocessing could
separate the cost from the benefit.

\paragraph{The assumptions question resolves in an unexpected
direction: there is no location benefit on the phase channel.}
The first sub-question --- the conditions under which the joint
observation of location invariance and tail movement implies
intrinsic coupling --- presupposed a location benefit delivered by
de-seasonalisation. The discriminating measurement (phase-asymmetry
correlation in demand space, comparable across specs because reported
in raw-demand units) was
\(\mathrm{corr}(\dd D/\dd t,\, e) = -0.247,\, -0.250,\, -0.217\)
for the global, month\,\(\times\)\,hour, and identity (no-transform)
specs respectively --- a range of \(0.033\) on a baseline of
\(-0.25\), including the no-transform extreme. The location-axis
signal is therefore not \emph{delivered} by de-seasonalisation; it
is already present in the raw demand at near-identical magnitude.
The two later sub-questions (which sub-step delivers the location
benefit; whether a different family separates the cost from the
benefit) collapse on this channel: there is no benefit on the phase
channel to separate from the cost.

\paragraph{The remaining question.} The phase-correlation
result does not settle whether de-seasonalisation contributes to
forecast performance through some other channel. The discriminating
test held the embedding dimension fixed at \(d = 3\) across all four
specs --- by design, to isolate the climatology effect --- and the
no-transform pipeline was not run end-to-end through the production
backtest (per-day-type elbow-selected dimensions, the registered
evaluation, leakage guards). The next experiment is end-to-end:
re-fit the per-day-type embedding dimension to raw demand by the same
elbow rule used for the seasonal pipeline, run the full backtest from
this configuration, and compare against the seasonal pipeline on a
multi-channel criterion. A single-axis comparison (e.g.\ \(\MAE\)
alone) is insufficient: de-seasonalisation acts on four estimator
layers simultaneously, and the channels through which it could
plausibly contribute to forecast value are not all summarised by
average error.

\paragraph{Pre-registered comparison criterion.} Removal of
de-seasonalisation is licensed only if \emph{all four} channels
match within their tolerances. Tolerances derive from the
materiality framework of Section~\ref{sec:caveat} where possible
(channel~1) and from the structural conditioning facts established
in the rebaseline (channel~2).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Forecast accuracy.} Compare \(\MAE\) and \(\MAPE\)
  on the same backtest window as the seasonal pipeline. Tolerance:
  \(|\Delta\MAE| < 8~\mathrm{MW}\) and \(|\Delta\MAPE| < 0.05\)
  percentage points (\(\approx 1\%\) of the seasonal pipeline's
  baseline values, the scale of Section~\ref{sec:caveat}'s
  materiality threshold \(\taumin\)).
  \item \textbf{Diffusion numerical viability.} The rebaseline
  established a usable-vs-unusable contrast (intra-day
  \(\kappa(\Sj) \in [6,\, 1072]\) vs.\ full-process up to
  \(\sim 10^{20}\)) \emph{within} the seasonal pipeline in z-units;
  ratio-based comparisons of \(\kappa(\Sj)\) \emph{across} pipelines
  are not unit-invariant (z-space and demand-space variances have
  different absolute scales). The channel-2 question is binary
  and per-pipeline: does \(\Sj\) support the downstream predictive
  law? Operationally, for each pipeline, fit the production
  \(\Sj\) at \(N = 60\) sampled anchors per day-type and check that
  multi-step variance propagation via the gate-validated augmentation
  produces finite predictive variance at all 24 horizons; report the
  fraction of anchors finite at every horizon. Tolerance: that
  fraction is \(\ge 99\%\) for \emph{both} pipelines independently
  (failure on either side is fatal regardless of the other).
  \item \textbf{Interval coverage, hour-stratified.} Compare empirical
  coverage of the nominal \(95\%\) Gaussian-proxy intervals on the
  same dev window. The seasonal pipeline's intervals are calibrated
  in z-space and re-multiplied by \(\sigma_{m,h}\) in demand-space;
  the raw-demand pipeline has no scale step and would deliver
  uniformly-scaled intervals, so an average-coverage comparison can
  hide systematic hour-of-day miscalibration (the seasonal pipeline
  narrows overnight, widens at peaks; raw does not). Stratify
  coverage by hour-of-day. Tolerance: per-hour
  \(\max_{h} |\text{cov}_{\mathrm{raw}}(h) -
  \text{cov}_{\mathrm{seasonal}}(h)| < 5\) percentage points
  \emph{and} \(|\overline{\text{cov}}_{\mathrm{raw}}
  - \overline{\text{cov}}_{\mathrm{seasonal}}| < 2\) percentage
  points (comparing two miscalibrations to each other, not either
  to nominal --- Section~\ref{sec:caveat}'s Gaussian-proxy caveat
  already warns of under-coverage in the seasonal pipeline).
  \item \textbf{Heavy-tail strength.} Compare propagation-aware
  Student-\(t\) shape \(\nuhat\) (Section~\ref{sec:caveat}). The
  seasonal pipeline gives \(\nuhat \approx 6.5\); the §3.2
  reconciliation argues part of the apparent heavy tail was a
  propagation artefact tied to the de-seasonalisation, so the raw
  pipeline should give \(\nuhat\) at least as Gaussian. Tolerance:
  \(\nuhat_{\mathrm{raw}} \ge \nuhat_{\mathrm{seasonal}}\) (the
  direction-of-effect check; no upper bound --- substantial
  improvement here is itself a finding refining the §3.2
  reconciliation).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Result of the four-channel comparison.} End-to-end
within-harness comparison was run as
\texttt{scratch/diagnose\_deseasonalisation\_removal.py} on the
2021--22 development-set window (exploratory). Per-day-type
elbow-rule dimensions:
seasonal \(\{d_{\mathrm{wkdy}}, d_{\mathrm{sat}}, d_{\mathrm{sun}}\}
= \{2, 4, 3\}\) on \(z\); no-transform \(\{3, 2, 4\}\) on raw \(D\).
\(n = 3168\) hours per pipeline on the same dev window.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Forecast: \(\MAE_{\mathrm{seasonal}} = 627\) MW,
  \(\MAPE_{\mathrm{seasonal}} = 3.86\%\) vs.\
  \(\MAE_{\mathrm{raw}} = 2317\) MW,
  \(\MAPE_{\mathrm{raw}} = 13.57\%\): \(|\Delta\MAE| = 1690\) MW,
  \(|\Delta\MAPE| = 9.71\) pp. \textbf{FAIL} (tolerances
  \(<8\) MW, \(<0.05\) pp).
  \item Diffusion viability: both pipelines fraction-finite-at-24-
  horizons \(= 1.00\) on \(60/60\) anchors per day-type. \textbf{PASS}.
  \item Demand-space \(95\%\) interval coverage (hour-stratified):
  seasonal avg \(38.4\%\), raw avg \(24.2\%\); per-hour worst gap
  \(38.6\) pp (h=16), average gap \(14.1\) pp; raw over-covers
  overnight (h=0--4 in \(76\)--\(100\%\)) and crashes to
  \(0\)--\(2\%\) at midday/peak (h=8--20), the signature of a
  missing demand-space scale step. \textbf{FAIL} (tolerances
  worst-hour \(<5\) pp, avg \(<2\) pp).
  \item Heavy-tail strength: seasonal \(\nuhat = 5.93\) (excess
  kurtosis \(3.10\)), raw \(\nuhat = \infty\) (excess kurtosis
  \(-1.14\)). The raw value passes the pre-registered rule
  \(\nuhat_{\mathrm{raw}} \ge \nuhat_{\mathrm{seasonal}}\), but is
  an artefact of the same defect the coverage channel detected:
  raw's standardised residuals collapse because the predictive
  variance is mis-scaled relative to demand-space residual scale,
  driving excess kurtosis negative. The heavy-tail channel does not
  corroborate the result independently.
  \textbf{PASS in form, artefact in substance}.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Verdict per the pre-registered all-channel rule: HOLD.}
The within-harness 627 MW / 3.86\% is the comparator; the
production-frozen \(\approx 786\) MW / \(4.78\%\) reported in
Section~\ref{sec:backtest} is on a separate harness and is not the
comparator here.

\paragraph{The no-transform-matches-seasonal reading did not fire.}
The pre-registered conditions for licensing removal (forecast
accuracy within tolerance; demand-space coverage hour-stratified
within tolerance) were not met; reported as a worked trail rather
than a finding.

\paragraph{The no-transform-is-worse reading fired, and the
forecast-performance channel localised at the interval-coverage
channel.} De-seasonalisation delivers forecast performance through
the demand-space scale calibration: the seasonal pipeline's intervals
(and the diffusion \(\Sj\) they rest on) track diurnal scale via
\(\sigma_{m,h}\), while raw demand does not expose this scale at the
embedding layer. The per-hour coverage signature --- raw over-covers
overnight (uniform-width intervals are too wide where demand is small
and stable) and crashes to zero coverage at midday/peak (uniform-width
intervals are not wide enough at the demand peaks the seasonal
pipeline implicitly normalises against) --- is the mechanistic
fingerprint of the missing scale step.

The follow-up question narrows. The forecast-performance
contribution is not on the location axis (the phase-asymmetry signal
is independent of de-seasonalisation) but on the scale axis. The
next question is whether the scale-calibration benefit can be
delivered without the heavy-tail cost --- candidates: scale-only
normalisation (divide by \(\sigma_{m,h}\) without level removal), an
additive seasonal model that hands a separate scale-conditioning
input to the diffusion fit, or a Fourier-decomposition scale layer.

\paragraph{Pre-registered candidate A: scale-only normalisation.}
The minimal perturbation is to keep the scale step that the
preceding experiment located as load-bearing and drop the level step
that is the candidate carrier of the heavy-tail cost:
\(z_{\mathrm{A}} = D / \sigma_{m,h}\), no mean subtraction. The
comparison applies the same diagnostic harness as the
seasonal-vs-raw test: elbow-selected dimension per day-type on
\(z_{\mathrm{A}}\), the same four-channel measurement at the same
pre-registered tolerances.

The pre-registered heavy-tail-channel interpretation rule from the
preceding experiment is refined: \emph{the heavy-tail channel is
informative only conditional on the coverage channel passing}. Raw
demand's heavy-tail-channel PASS was an artefact of the same
mis-scaling the coverage channel detected (the standardised residuals
collapsed because the predictive variance was mis-scaled relative to
the realised residual scale); this pattern is general. For the
scale-only test and any future variant: if the coverage channel
fails, the heavy-tail channel reports ``not interpretable'' rather
than PASS or FAIL.

The experiment is also pre-committed to flag a design confound:
if the elbow rule picks a dimension for \(z_{\mathrm{A}}\) outside
the range \([2, 4]\) that the seasonal and raw pipelines landed in,
the comparison is contaminated by a dimension change rather than
testing the additive-step removal, and the candidate-A verdict is
withheld pending a decision on whether to constrain \(d\) or accept
the confound.

Three outcomes are pre-stated. The \emph{all-channels-pass outcome}:
the additive sub-step is the removable lever; the scale-only
pipeline delivers the scale-calibration benefit at reduced tail
cost; the §3 caveat becomes specific to the additive step. The
\emph{coverage-passes-but-tail-fails outcome}: the heavy-tail cost
attaches to the multiplicative \(\sigma_{m,h}\) step, not the
additive one; scale-only is not the lever; the engineering question
moves to alternative scale-conditioning candidates. The
\emph{accuracy-fails outcome} (forecast accuracy noticeably worse
despite the coverage channel holding): the additive step is also
conditioning the embedding-state input to the local-linear map
(centring the lag state), not just removing a level; the
cost-vs-benefit decomposition is not separable within this family
at the level the four-channel diagnostic can probe, and the
engineering question moves from ``which sub-step'' to ``is the
additive-and-multiplicative combination irreducibly coupled.''
\paragraph{Candidate-A result: the accuracy-fails outcome fires;
verdict HOLD.}
Scale-only \(z_{\mathrm{A}} = D / \sigma_{m,h}\) was run end-to-end
through the same diagnostic harness as the seasonal-vs-raw
comparison
(\texttt{scratch/run\_candidate\_A\_only.py}, dev 2021--22,
\(n = 3168\)). Per-day-type elbow dimension is \(d = 4\) across all
day-types, inside the pre-registered \([2,4]\) confound band.
Three-pipeline forecast accuracy, side-by-side:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r}
\toprule
Pipeline & \(\MAE\) (MW) & \(\MAPE\) (\%) \\
\midrule
Seasonal      & \(627\) & \(3.86\) \\
Scale-only    & \(1951\) & \(11.98\) \\
No-transform & \(2317\) & \(13.57\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Scale-only recovers some of the gap closed by the seasonal pipeline
(\(\Delta\MAPE\) reduced from \(9.71\) pp under no-transform to
\(8.12\) pp under scale-only) but remains \(\approx 3.1\times\) the
seasonal pipeline's error and well outside the pre-registered
accuracy-channel tolerance (\(|\Delta\MAE| < 8\) MW,
\(|\Delta\MAPE| < 0.05\) pp). The accuracy-fails outcome fires: the
additive sub-step is also conditioning the embedding-state input to
the local-linear map, not just removing a level. The cost-vs-benefit
decomposition is \emph{not separable at the four-channel diagnostic's
resolution within this parametric family}.
\emph{Mechanistic-attribution caveat:} the scale-only elbow rule
selected \(d = 4\) uniformly across day-types while the seasonal
pipeline uses \(\{d_{\mathrm{wkdy}}, d_{\mathrm{sat}},
d_{\mathrm{sun}}\} = \{2, 4, 3\}\); the attribution to the additive
sub-step is therefore partially confounded with the dimension shift
(weekday \(+2\), saturday \(0\), sunday \(+1\)). The HOLD verdict on
removal is robust to this confound --- whether the mechanism is
missing-\(\mu\) or wrong-\(d\) or both, the practical conclusion is
the same. To isolate the additive sub-step's contribution the
scale-only pipeline is re-run with the embedding dimension forced to
the seasonal assignment \(\{2, 4, 3\}\), making the additive step
the only remaining difference between the two pipelines. Three
\(\MAPE\)-based outcomes are pre-stated for this disambiguation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Dimension-driven outcome.}
  \(\MAPE_{\{2,4,3\}} < 5\%\): scale-only at the seasonal
  dimensions recovers the forecast performance the elbow-selected
  dimensions did not, and the attribution to the
  additive sub-step is overclaimed. The additive step's marginal
  contribution to forecast accuracy is smaller than the
  candidate-A result suggested.
  \item \textbf{Both-contribute outcome.}
  \(5\% \le \MAPE_{\{2,4,3\}} < 9\%\): both the dimension shift
  and the additive sub-step contribute substantively to the
  candidate-A failure. The additive-step attribution is partially
  supported but not the sole story.
  \item \textbf{Additive-step-driven outcome.}
  \(\MAPE_{\{2,4,3\}} \ge 9\%\): forcing the seasonal dimensions
  does not materially reduce the failure; the additive step is
  doing real work independent of dimension.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Disambiguation result: additive-step-driven outcome.}
Scale-only at the seasonal dimensions \(\{2,4,3\}\) gives
\(\MAE_{\{2,4,3\}} = 2158.6\) MW, \(\MAPE_{\{2,4,3\}} = 13.13\%\) on
the same dev set --- \emph{worse} than scale-only at the
elbow-selected \(\{4,4,4\}\) (\(\MAPE = 11.98\%\)), and only
\(0.4\) pp better than the no-transform pipeline
(\(\MAPE = 13.57\%\)) --- the additive-step-driven outcome fires:
the additive sub-step does real work independent of dimension. The
four-point ranking at matched dimensions is \(3.86\% \to 13.13\%
\to 13.57\%\) as one drops first
the additive \(\mu_{m,h}\) step and then the multiplicative
\(\sigma_{m,h}\) step --- the additive step alone accounts for
\(\approx 9.3\) pp of the \(\approx 9.7\) pp total cost. Forcing the
elbow's preferred \(d = 4\) on weekday and sunday recovers
\(\approx 1.1\) pp; that is small relative to the additive step's
contribution and does not overturn the accuracy-fails reading. The
mechanistic-attribution caveat in the preceding paragraph is
therefore resolved: the additive-sub-step attribution is not a
dimension artifact.

The other channels: the diffusion-viability channel PASSes
(scale-only \(60/60\) finite per day-type, identical to seasonal);
the coverage channel reports average coverage \(41.10\%\) vs.\
seasonal's \(38.35\%\), \(|\Delta\overline{\mathrm{cov}}|
= 2.75\) pp, FAIL on the average-gap tolerance (worst-hour test
deferred --- the accuracy channel already settled the verdict); the
heavy-tail channel gives \(\nuhat_{\mathrm{scale}} = 5.27\) vs.\
\(\nuhat_{\mathrm{seasonal}} = 5.93\), reported as a
\emph{directional observation only}: the pre-registered
coverage-conditional rule applies (the coverage channel failed, so
the heavy-tail channel is not interpretable as a pass/fail signal).
The directional sign --- scale-only is slightly more heavy-tailed
than seasonal --- is contrary to the mechanistic story that the
additive step \emph{carries} the heavy-tail cost; if anything,
removing the additive step makes the tail slightly worse rather
than better. This weakens (but does not by itself rule out) the
original coverage-passes-but-tail-fails hypothesis that the cost
attaches entirely to the multiplicative step.

\paragraph{Resolved standing position.} The \(z\)-score coupling
carries a documented heavy-tail cost (Section~\ref{sec:caveat}); a
demand-space-scale-calibration contribution to forecast performance
(localised at the coverage channel in the seasonal-vs-raw experiment);
and an additive-level-removal contribution to forecast performance
(localised by this candidate-A experiment under the
accuracy-fails outcome). All three
ride on the same parametric object \((\mu_{m,h}, \sigma_{m,h})\). At
the four-channel diagnostic's resolution within this family, the
cost and the two benefit channels are intertwined: removing either
sub-step degrades the accuracy channel substantially. The
engineering question moves out of the additive-vs-multiplicative
decomposition; the next-tier analysis (a separate
continuous-climatology preprocessing, and the framework-level
enumeration of the available levers against the heavy-tail cost) is
the subject of Section~\ref{sec:qualifier-verdict}. The
phase-asymmetry finding that motivated this section's investigation
is independent of the de-seasonalisation step.

\subsection{Available levers against the heavy-tail cost: framework
enumeration and empirical landing}
\label{sec:qualifier-verdict}

The Section~\ref{sec:caveat} caveat documents two ways \(\Pij\)
departs from \(\kQ\) (phase-mis-centred mean; Gaussian-proxy
diffusion). The Section~\ref{sec:zscore} investigation establishes
the \(z\)-score as a framework coupling rather than benign
preprocessing, and ends at the question of how to engineer around
the heavy-tail cost. The analysis has three layers, and conflating
them misreads either the framework or the empirical landing.

\emph{Framework.} The \(\kQ\) programme permits kernel-form,
state-augmentation, and per-horizon relaxations; its law-agnostic
content supports four design options --- a Student-\(t\) local
kernel; state augmentation within the lagged-\(z\) embedding;
per-horizon direct fitting; and the combined state-augmentation +
kernel-form variant. Rebaseline-grade evidence (per-anchor library
kurt \(24\)--\(33\)) motivates the search.

\emph{Empirical contamination magnitude.} A Patra--Sen
consistent two-component-mixture estimator~\cite{PatraSen2016},
with the predicted-Gaussian as the known component, applied to
pooled standardised per-anchor library residuals from the
production estimation path, places at least \(49\)--\(74\%\) of
the per-anchor library mass in the non-Gaussian mixture component
across day-types and preprocessings (\(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%}\), a
conservative lower bound by their Lemma~4). A pipeline-aware
Gaussian-innovation null through the identical protocol gives
\(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} = 0.002\); the maximum value any single
heavy-tailed AR(1) reproduces, across both population-kurt-matched
and sample-kurt-matched checks, is \(0.26\). The contamination is
mixture-structured, not a small heavy-tail perturbation, and is
preprocessing-independent.

\emph{Empirical activation of the framework levers.} On the
development set at \(n = 132\), only the Student-\(t\) kernel-form
lever delivers a statistically clean log-loss improvement on
routine-day data (\(\approx 1.8\%\) mid-horizon, CI excludes zero);
state augmentation, per-horizon direct fitting, and the combined
variant are null. The improvement is modest --- well below the
contamination magnitude the previous paragraph reports. The only
consistent reading of large contamination mass alongside
modest-to-null empirical levers is that the contamination is
structurally orthogonal to the lagged-\(z\) state on which all four
levers operate. The licensed direction is exogenous information ---
temperature, weather regime, calendar event flags --- not
state-augmentation or kernel-form changes within the past-\(z\)
information set.

\subsection{Out-of-sample backtest}
\label{sec:backtest}

The iterated one-step predictor is backtested on Ontario ground truth
for complete delivery days strictly after the model-specification
cutoff. Leakage guard: fits and \(z\)-score climatology use only
\(\le\)-cutoff data; post-cutoff actuals enter only as query history
and scoring truth. With forecast \(\hat{y}_{d,h}\) and actual
\(y_{d,h}\) over delivery days \(d \in \mathcal{D}\) and horizons
\(h \in \{1, \ldots, 24\}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mae-mape}
\MAE \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|\cdot 24}
\sum_{d \in \mathcal{D}} \sum_{h=1}^{24}
\bigl| \hat{y}_{d,h} - y_{d,h} \bigr|,
\qquad
\MAPE \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|\cdot 24}
\sum_{d \in \mathcal{D}} \sum_{h=1}^{24}
\frac{\bigl| \hat{y}_{d,h} - y_{d,h} \bigr|}{y_{d,h}} \,.
\end{equation}
In a day-ahead forecast, horizon \(h\) is collinear with hour-of-day,
so the error structure is \emph{diurnal} (deep night easiest;
dawn/dusk ramps hardest), not pure horizon compounding. The recorded
backtest gives \(\MAE = 786~\mathrm{MW}\),
\(\MAPE = 4.78\%\) over \(|\mathcal{D}| = 500\) out-of-sample
days, \(\approx 1.5\times\) better than seasonal persistence at
every hour. The number is bound to the recorded artifact by its
content hash;\footnote{Body sha256 \texttt{e405a121\(\ldots\)};
recorded under frozen-spec hash \texttt{f0dd9c87\(\ldots\)}
(\(\mu_{m,h}\) climatology). The per-horizon table appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:backtest}.}
interval coverage carries the Section~\ref{sec:caveat}
Gaussian-proxy caveat: a Gaussian interval around a heavy-tailed
innovation under-covers.

\paragraph{Climatology comparison.} The seasonal coupling
(Section~\ref{sec:zscore}) raised an engineering question: does the
\((\mu_{m,h}, \sigma_{m,h})\) lookup's piecewise-constant structure,
which produces discrete climatology jumps at month boundaries
(twelve delivery days per year), impose a forecast-accuracy cost
relative to a continuous alternative? An end-to-end Fourier
climatology --- the continuous basis in (day-of-year, hour-of-day)
with \(K_{\mathrm{year}} = K_{\mathrm{day}} = 8\), no
interaction terms --- was re-frozen as an alternative predictor
spec and re-run on the same \(500\)-day post-cutoff span. The
two CLAIM-grade backtest artifacts compare as follows:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r}
\toprule
metric & \(\mu_{m,h}\) lookup & Fourier (\(K_y=K_d=8\)) & \(\Delta\) \\
\midrule
overall \(\MAE\)            & \(786\)~MW   & \(904\)~MW   & \(+118\)~MW \\
overall \(\MAPE\)           & \(4.78\%\)   & \(5.42\%\)   & \(+0.64\)~pp \\
persistence-168h \(\MAE\)   & \(1138\)~MW  & \(1138\)~MW  & --- \\

The \(\mu_{m,h}\) lookup wins overall by \(\sim 15\%\) in \(\MAE\)
and \(0.64\) percentage points in \(\MAPE\). Diurnal breakdown
locates the difference: Fourier wins on the dawn ramp (h~=~7--9:
\(-57, -118, -48\) MW) but loses substantially during the daytime
peak (h~=~11--16: \(+161\) to \(+361\) MW). The daytime-peak losses
dominate because demand --- and therefore the absolute error in
megawatts --- is high there. The mechanism is straightforward: the
\((\mu_{m,h}, \sigma_{m,h})\) lookup has \(288\) independent cells,
which carry the day-of-year \(\times\) hour-of-day interaction
explicitly (March-12:00 differs from June-12:00 because the
seasonal peak-load shape varies by month). The Fourier model
without cross-interaction terms cannot represent this structure
(\(17\) day-of-year \(\times\) \(17\) hour-of-day independent
parameters, no products). The seam contamination identified in
Section~\ref{sec:zscore} is real but small in operational
consequence: at production scale, the \(\sim 12\) month-boundary
days per year on which Fourier improves do not outweigh the
\(\sim 365\) days on which it loses the seasonal-by-hour
resolution.\footnote{Fourier artifact body sha256

\subsection{Registered forward prediction experiment}
\label{sec:registered}

A fair retrospective IESO comparison is not recoverable
(Section~\ref{sec:ieso}), so the comparison is run forward. The model
specification (estimator identity, resolved hyperparameters, data
cutoff \(t_{\mathrm{cut}}\), code commit, and a content hash of the
\(\le t_{\mathrm{cut}}\) actuals) is committed before any forecast
is issued, and the same content hash is re-checked at every
prediction-time load so the recorded specification cannot be silently
edited. The predictor is local/lazy (per-anchor fit at prediction
time from \(\le t_{\mathrm{cut}}\) library data), so the committed
specification pins the function and its inputs; a cutoff guard blocks
any post-\(t_{\mathrm{cut}}\) data from entering a fit.
Forecasts issue forward; scraped actuals settle into an append-only
ledger (IESO restates recent demand --- revisions appended, never
overwritten); scoring runs against persistence baselines with IESO
published values recorded as differently-horizoned context.

\subsection{The retrospective IESO head-to-head is infeasible from
public archives}
\label{sec:ieso}

Can the forecast be compared retrospectively against IESO's own
published forecast on historical settled days? A fair comparison
requires two properties of an IESO archive: (i) the values reported
must be on the Ontario-demand basis (matching the forecast target
of this paper), and (ii) the values reported must be the day-ahead
issue (matching its horizon). The simpler hypothesis is that some
public IESO archive supplies both. To test it, all 133 public IESO
report directories were enumerated and each checked against the two
properties.

The simpler hypothesis fails, via two independent blockers. The
Ontario-demand-basis archive (Adequacy3
\texttt{ForecastOntDemand}) retains, for days old enough to have
settled actuals, only the end-of-delivery-day version --- so its
horizon is wrong (property (ii) violated). The day-ahead-issued
archives (DATotals, PredispTotals) publish only market totals --- so
their basis is wrong (property (i) violated): ``Total Load'' carries
a definitional \(\approx +2485~\mathrm{MW}\) offset over Ontario
Demand (MarketQuantity enumeration: Total
Energy/Loss/Load/Dispatchable/10S/10N/30R; none is Ontario demand).
No archive satisfies both. The retrospective comparison is
infeasible from public data; hence the registered forward design
of Section~\ref{sec:registered}. (Stated for public archives as of
the recorded access date.)

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Estimator limitations (Section~\ref{sec:caveat}).}
  \(\Pij\) departs from \(\kQ\) in location (\(\Cj\)
  phase-mis-centred) and shape (\(\Sj\) Gaussian proxy of a
  heavy-tailed innovation). The pre-registered decomposition bounds
  recoverable loss at \(\approx 4.8\%\) and \(\approx 2.3\%\) of dev
  log-loss; \textbf{neither clears the pre-registered 1\% materiality
  bar} (heavy-tail interval includes zero). Multi-step propagation
  softens the apparent kurtosis \(\approx 4\times\); the one-step
  conditional law remains heavy-tailed at the library-residual level.

  \item \textbf{Available within-\(z\) levers tested
  (Section~\ref{sec:qualifier-verdict}).} The kernel-form
  (Student-\(t\)), state-augmentation (within lagged \(z\)),
  per-horizon direct fit, and combined relaxations of the
  Section~\ref{sec:caveat} qualifiers are all framework-permitted
  and were tested empirically. Only the kernel-form lever delivers
  a statistically clean log-loss improvement, and the effect is
  modest (\(\approx 1.8\%\) reduction at mid horizons). State
  augmentation and combined variants are null.

  \item \textbf{Single-step only.} The semigroup \(\{\Pi_t\}\) is not
  constructed; multi-step is iteration of \(\Pidelta\), validated for
  error growth. A semigroup treatment is open.

  \item \textbf{Exogenous information.} No weather/exogenous
  covariates. The Patra--Sen quantification places at least
  \(49\)--\(74\%\) of per-anchor library mass in a non-Gaussian
  mixture component, and the within-\(z\) levers above do not access
  it; the licensed direction for accommodating the heavy-tailed
  conditional law is exogenous information rather than further
  variation within the \(z\) information set.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Recorded results carry two content
hashes: an inputs hash (git commit, model-specification hash, library
versions, RNG seeds, declared inputs) and a body hash recomputed at
read time. The recording refuses to write from a dirty source tree.
Exploratory development-set material is marked as such and is not
cited as a result elsewhere in the paper.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The two ways \(\Pij\) departs from \(\kQ\) --- phase-mis-centred mean
and Gaussian-proxy diffusion --- were pre-registered for elimination
and both fall below the materiality bar on the validated full
population. The framework-level enumeration in
Section~\ref{sec:qualifier-verdict} confirms that the
\(\kQ\) programme \emph{permits} kernel-form, state-augmentation, and
per-horizon relaxations of these qualifiers; the empirical
comparison on the \(n = 132\) development set finds only the
kernel-form lever statistically clean (\(\approx 1.8\%\) mid-horizon
log-loss reduction), with state augmentation and combined variants
null. A Patra--Sen contamination quantification places at least
\(49\)--\(74\%\) of per-anchor library mass in a non-Gaussian
mixture component --- substantial mass that the within-\(z\) levers
do not access. The combined reading is that the contamination is
structurally orthogonal to the lagged-\(z\) state on which the
levers operate. The licensed direction for accommodating the
heavy-tailed conditional law is therefore \emph{exogenous
information} (temperature, weather regime, calendar event flags),
not further variation within the \(z\) information set.
