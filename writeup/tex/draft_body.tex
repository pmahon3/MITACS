\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

This paper frames short-horizon Ontario electricity-demand forecasting
as a question about the \emph{conditional law of a reconstructed
dynamical state}: given a delay embedding of recent demand, what is
the distribution of the next state? A local-Gaussian
conditional-kernel estimator of that law is instantiated on IESO data
and evaluated as a \emph{prediction experiment} --- with an
out-of-sample backtest, a registered forward experiment, and an
accounting of comparability against IESO's own published forecasts.

The estimator targets the conditional-regularity kernel \(\kQ\)
determined by the data measure via Rokhlin disintegration
(Section~\ref{sec:method}).

\section{Theory and method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Construction}
\label{sec:construction}

\paragraph{Embedding and library.}
Let \(s_t\) be a scalar observable (here, de-seasonalised hourly
demand; Section~\ref{sec:pipeline}) sampled on a regular grid
\(t \in \mathbb{Z}\). The delay embedding into \(\Rb^{d}\) is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:embed}
x_t \;=\; (s_t,\; s_{t-1},\; \ldots,\; s_{t-(d-1)}) \,.
\end{equation}
Per day-type \(\mathrm{dt} \in \{\mathrm{weekday},
\mathrm{saturday}, \mathrm{sunday}\}\) the embedding dimension
\(d = d(\mathrm{dt})\) is selected on pre-cutoff library data by the
elbow rule on the one-step in-sample prediction-skill curve
\(\rho \mapsto d\). The pre-cutoff library is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:library}
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \{\,(x_t, x_{t+1}) : t \le t_{\mathrm{cut}},\;
t \text{ on the day-type } \mathrm{dt}\,\} \,,
\end{equation}
with \(t_{\mathrm{cut}}\) the model-specification cutoff.

\paragraph{Local linear fit.}
For a query state \(x \in \Rb^{d}\), form the S-map kernel
weights~\cite{Sugihara1994}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weights}
w_i(x;\theta) \;=\;
\exp\!\left(-\theta\,\frac{\lVert x_i - x \rVert}{\bar d(x)}\right),
\qquad
\bar d(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}}
\lVert x_i - x \rVert,
\qquad (x_i, y_i) \in \mathcal{L},
\end{equation}
with \(\theta \ge 0\) dimensionless: \(\theta = 0\) gives
\(w_i \equiv 1\) (uniform weights, the global linear fit) and
\(\theta\) increasing localises the weights toward the nearest
neighbours. \(y_i \equiv x_{t_i + 1}\). The
For each query state \(x\) and bandwidth \(\theta \ge 0\), the
weighted least-squares local linear one-step operator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wls}
C(x;\theta) \;=\;
\arg\min_{C \in \Rb^{d \times d}}
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} w_i(x;\theta) \, \lVert y_i - x_i C \rVert^{2}
\;=\; \bigl(X^{\top} W(\theta) X\bigr)^{-1} X^{\top} W(\theta) Y,
\end{equation}
with \(W(\theta) = \operatorname{diag}(w_i(x;\theta))\), \(X, Y\) the
stacked library pairs, and \(y_i \equiv x_{t_i + 1}\). Note that
\(C(x;\theta)\) is a \emph{family} of operators indexed by \(\theta\):
\(\theta = 0\) gives the global linear fit (uniform weights), and
\(\theta\) increasing localises the operator at \(x\). The one-step
map is then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:drift}
x_{\text{next}} \;\approx\; x \cdot C(x;\theta) \qquad
\text{(row convention).}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Bandwidth: a simplex-interpolated \(\theta\)-field.}
The bandwidth is not a free hyperparameter to be selected at the
query; it is itself a \emph{state-dependent quantity},
\(\theta = \theta(x)\), produced by a two-stage scheme.

\emph{Stage 1 (fit-time, one per library anchor).}
At each library point \(x_j \in \mathcal{L}\), select \(\theta^{*}_j\)
by true leave-one-out cross-validation on one-step squared error,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loocv}
\theta^{*}_j \;=\;
\arg\min_{\theta \ge 0}\;
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LOO}}(x_j;\theta), \qquad
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LOO}}(x_j;\theta)
\;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}}
\bigl\lVert y_i - x_i C^{(-i)}(x_j;\theta) \bigr\rVert^{2},
\end{equation}
with \(C^{(-i)}(x_j;\theta)\) the WLS fit at query \(x_j\) excluding
\((x_i, y_i)\). The collection
\(\{(x_j, \theta^{*}_j) : x_j \in \mathcal{L}\}\) is a discrete
\(\theta\)-field over the pre-cutoff embedding, frozen at fit time.

\emph{Stage 2 (query-time, per prediction).}
At a query state \(x\), interpolate the field via the
\((d+1)\)-simplex of nearest library neighbours,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:simplextheta}
\theta(x) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{d+1} \pi_k(x)\, \theta^{*}_{j_k(x)},
\end{equation}
where \(\{j_k(x)\}_{k=1}^{d+1}\) are the indices of the \(d+1\) nearest
library points to \(x\), and \(\pi_k(x)\) are normalised Sugihara
simplex weights
\(\pi_k(x) \propto \exp(-\lVert x_{j_k(x)} - x\rVert / d_{\min}(x))\)
with \(d_{\min}(x) = \min_k \lVert x_{j_k(x)} - x\rVert\). The
field thus transports a state-dependent bandwidth from the library to
any query.

\emph{The operator at \(x\) is then a single evaluation of the
family,}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Cofx}
C(x) \;:=\; C\bigl(x;\,\theta(x)\bigr),
\end{equation}
which inherits the \(\theta\)-field's smoothness on the embedding.
The plain residual covariance at this fit,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:diff}
\Sigma(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} \bigl(r_i - \bar r\bigr)
\bigl(r_i - \bar r\bigr)^{\!\top},
\qquad r_i := y_i - x_i C(x),
\quad \bar r := \tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{L}|}\sum_i r_i,
\end{equation}
defines the diffusion. Together they specify the state-dependent
Gaussian Markov kernel
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kernel}
\Pi(x, \cdot) \;=\; \Nrm\!\bigl(x \, C(x),\; \Sigma(x)\bigr).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Why the plain (unweighted) covariance.}
A weighted residual covariance \(\Sigma^{w}(x) = \sum_i w_i\,
(r_i - \bar r)(r_i - \bar r)^{\!\top} / \sum_i w_i\) shrinks toward
the small-residual core as \(\theta(x)\) grows --- a collapse
pathology the kernel-weighting machinery induces but does not
represent in the estimand. The plain covariance is the unbiased local
second moment of the residuals selected into the fit by \(w\), free
of that shrinkage.

\paragraph{The conditional law \(\kQ\) the estimator targets.}
On a probability space \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)\) with two
observations \(Q, F\), the Rokhlin disintegration
theorem~\cite{Rokhlin1952,Bogachev2007} produces a Markov kernel
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kappaQ}
\kQ(q, \cdot) \;=\; P(F \in \cdot \mid Q = q),
\end{equation}
determined by the measure --- not posited as an additional dynamical
law. Under temporal indexing of the observations, finite-dimensional
distributions are coherent, so the kernels compose: the
Chapman--Kolmogorov equation is a consequence, not a hypothesis, and
yields a Markov semigroup \(\{\Pi_t\}\)~\cite{Eisner2015}. The
companion Koopman--Perron duality
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:KP}
\int K_t\, g\, \dd\mu \;=\; \int g\, \dd(P_t^{*}\mu)
\end{equation}
relates the Koopman operator \(K_t g = g \circ T_t\) (when the
dynamics is deterministic, recovering the classical Koopman operator
when the kernels are Dirac~\cite{LasotaMackey1994}) to its
Perron--Frobenius adjoint \(P_t^{*}\) on densities. Three layers are
useful to keep notationally distinct: \(\kQ\) (the disintegration
kernel; a conditional law), \(\{\Pi_t\}\) (the time-indexed Markov
semigroup), and \(K_t\) (the operator-layer object).

\paragraph{The estimator's relationship to \(\kQ\).}
The \(\{\Pi_t\}\) semigroup and the operator family
\(\{K_t\} / \{P_t^*\}\) exist in the data measure by the construction
of Section~\ref{sec:multistep}'s framework reference: stationarity of
the de-seasonalised series and the delay-embedding observation
structure together satisfy the temporal coherence that forces the
Chapman--Kolmogorov property; the disintegration kernel \(\kQ\)
admits the family, derived from the measure rather than posited.
The object Section~\ref{sec:construction} constructs is a
finite-sample, parametric, Gaussian-form estimator of the
\emph{single-step slice} \(\Pidelta\) of that family at the
delay-embedding layer. From the estimated \((C(x), \Sigma(x))\) the
operator-layer slice \(K_{\Delta} / P_{\Delta}^{*}\) is recoverable
in the non-Dirac regime (\(\Sj \neq 0\)).
\(\Cj\) is a linearisation of the conditional-mean factor; \(\Sj\) is
the second moment of \(\kQ\)'s Gaussian projection; \(\Sj \to 0\)
reproduces the classical-Koopman (deterministic) collapse. Three
qualifiers bound this correspondence:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Register.} \(\kQ\) is determined by the measure; this
  work \emph{constructs} a Gaussian proxy and fits it from finite
  data --- ``estimator of,'' not ``instance of.''

  \item \textbf{Imposed Gaussianity.} \(\Pij = \Nrm(x \Cj, \Sj)\)
  coincides with \(\kQ\) only where the local conditional law is
  itself Gaussian. Section~\ref{sec:caveat} quantifies the gap as
  real but small in recoverable forecast loss.

  \item \textbf{Scope of extraction.} What is extracted is the
  single-step slice \(\Pidelta\); other slices
  \(\Pi_{h\Delta}\) of the same data-measure semigroup
  \(\{\Pi_t\}\) are not estimated directly. The multi-step
  propagation modes of Section~\ref{sec:multistep} extend
  \(\widehat{\Pidelta}\) to \(h\) steps by iteration; whether the
  iterate \((\widehat{\Pidelta})^{h}\) tracks the directly-extractable
  \(\widehat{\Pi}_{h\Delta}\) is a separable empirical question
  testable by a Chapman--Kolmogorov consistency check at one or more
  discrete horizons. The check is reported as a synthetic gate in
  Section~\ref{sec:synth} and as an in-sample Ontario probe in
  Section~\ref{sec:limits}. Existence of \(\{\Pi_t\}\) is a property
  of the measure; access to it beyond \(\Pidelta\) is a property of
  the extraction scheme.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-step propagation: mean iteration and SMC}
\label{sec:multistep}

The one-step kernel \(\Pij\) admits two distinct \(h\)-step
propagation modes. Both \emph{iterate} the estimated single-step
\(\widehat{\Pidelta}\) to extend it to \(h\) steps. The data-measure
semigroup \(\{\Pi_t\}\) and its slices \(\Pi_{h\Delta}\) at horizons
\(h > 1\) exist in the measure (see qualifier~3 above and
Section~\ref{sec:construction}); whether the iterate
\((\widehat{\Pidelta})^{h}\) agrees with the directly-extractable
slice \(\widehat{\Pi}_{h\Delta}\) is an empirical
Chapman--Kolmogorov-consistency question, separable from the
iteration scheme itself.

\emph{Mean iteration} (point trajectory). Starting from
\(x_0\), produce a deterministic length-\(h\) trajectory by iterating
the conditional mean,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:meaniter}
x_h^{\mathrm{mean}} \;=\;
x_{h-1}^{\mathrm{mean}} \cdot C\!\bigl(x_{h-1}^{\mathrm{mean}}\bigr),
\qquad h = 1, 2, \ldots
\end{equation}
The diffusion \(\Sj\) sizes a per-step interval but never feeds
forward; the point forecast at horizon \(h\) is
\(\hat{x}_h = x_h^{\mathrm{mean}}\).

\emph{Sequential Monte Carlo}~\cite{DoucetJohansen2009}
(ensemble of trajectories). Maintain \(M\) particles
\(\{x_h^{(m)}\}_{m=1}^{M}\); at each step, draw each particle's next
state from the local kernel at its current position,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:smc-step}
x_h^{(m)} \;\sim\; \Pi\!\bigl(x_{h-1}^{(m)}, \cdot\bigr)
\;=\; \Nrm\!\bigl(x_{h-1}^{(m)} \cdot C\!\bigl(x_{h-1}^{(m)}\bigr),\;
\Sigma\!\bigl(x_{h-1}^{(m)}\bigr)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The Gaussian form of Eq.~\ref{eq:smc-step} samples from the
estimator's Gaussian projection of \(\kQ\); an alternative
\emph{empirical-residual} variant resamples a coordinate-0 residual
\(r^{(m)}\) directly from the local fit's residual array and sets
\(x_h^{(m)} = x_{h-1}^{(m)} \cdot C(x_{h-1}^{(m)}) + r^{(m)}\)
(rank-1: only coord 0 is stochastic; coords 1..d-1 are deterministic
lag shifts). Both variants iterate \(\Pidelta\); they differ only in
which sampling law the per-step innovation is drawn from.
Point forecast at horizon \(h\) is the ensemble mean
\(\hat{x}_h = M^{-1}\sum_m x_h^{(m)}\); predictive intervals are
ensemble quantiles.

The two modes differ in their use of the diffusion factor \(\Sj\).
Mean iteration discards \(\Sj\) at each step; SMC propagates it
(or, in the empirical variant, the residuals it summarises).
Qualifier 2 (imposed Gaussianity; see Section~\ref{sec:caveat})
therefore has asymmetric consequences across the two modes: a
structurally heavy-tailed innovation enters the mean-iteration point
forecast only through its effect on \(C\), but enters the SMC
predictive distribution directly at every step.

\section{Structural caveat: a mis-centred conditional mean and a
Gaussian-proxy diffusion}
\label{sec:caveat}

The two estimator factors \(\Cj\) and \(\Sj\) each carry a quantified
limitation. A decomposition on the 2021--22 development-set window,
with predictive variance propagated over the full delivery-day
population, finds the recoverable-loss factors small in both
directions; the specific values are deferred to the
Section~\ref{sec:caveat-decomposition} reporting and inherit the
parameterisation caveat noted at Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\paragraph{\(\Sj\) is rank-1 by construction.} For a single-variable
delay embedding, coordinates \(2, \ldots, d\) of the one-step image
are deterministic shifts of the input coordinates
(\(Y_{[:,\,1:]} = X_{[:,\,:-1]}\) exactly). The only stochastic
content of the one-step map is the scalar coordinate-0 innovation;
therefore \(\Sj\) is rank-1 by construction, independent of data ---
the multivariate diffusion is structurally a single scalar innovation
variance embedded in \(\Rb^{d \times d}\).

\paragraph{That scalar innovation is non-Gaussian.} The
non-Gaussianity question is therefore \emph{univariate}. The
estimator, validated against synthetic ground truth
(Section~\ref{sec:synth}), gives a heavy-tailed one-step innovation.

\paragraph{The conditional mean is phase-mis-centred.}
The conditional-mean factor is not faithful either. The embedding
gives the local linear map no phase information --- it cannot tell a
state rising through a demand level from one falling through it ---
so it centres between rising and falling regimes and carries a
structured residual bias. The signature is a correlation between the
signed \(z\)-space error and the demand derivative \(\dd z/\dd t\):
measured, it is \(\mathrm{corr} = -0.246\) (bootstrap 95\% CI
\([-0.283,\, -0.207]\), \(n = 3167\)), monotone across
rising/falling phase. \(\Pij\)'s location, not only its shape,
departs from \(\kQ\).%
\footnote{Measured under the predecessor Gaussian-kernel
parameterisation; the structural claim is a property of the
embedding (no phase information) and is parameterisation-independent.
See the umbrella note at Section~\ref{sec:limits}.}

\paragraph{Decomposition.}
\label{sec:caveat-decomposition}
A diagnostic on the 2021--22 development-set window bounds the
recoverable loss of each factor by oracle counterfactuals on the
multi-step forecast log-loss \(\Lbar\), over all delivery days, with
predictive variance propagated across the embedding-dimension change.
The recoverable-loss factors are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:DA-DB}
\DA \;:=\; \frac{\Lbar - \Lbar^{(A)}}{\Lbar},
\qquad
\DB \;:=\; \frac{\Lbar - \Lbar^{(B)}}{\Lbar},
\end{equation}
where \(\Lbar^{(A)}\) is the log-loss under the oracle that removes
the phase-conditional mean bias, and \(\Lbar^{(B)}\) is the loss
under the oracle that replaces the Gaussian innovation law with a
Student-\(t\) fitted on the residuals (shape \(\nuhat\)).
On the full delivery-day population the recoverable loss is small in
both directions: \(\DA = 0.048\) (95\% day-bootstrap CI
\([0.007,\,0.086]\)) and \(\DB = 0.023\) (CI
\([-0.000,\,0.048]\), including zero), at \(\nuhat = 6.5\). Neither
effect is large or cleanly established enough to act on; restricting
to the single-dimension subpopulation (\(\approx 58\%\) of days)
gives larger point estimates (\(\DA \approx 0.061\),
\(\DB \approx 0.091\)) but overlapping intervals, the same
qualitative reading. The diffusion-shape diagnostic is run on
standardised residuals of the multi-step forecast under
propagation-aware variance; on the same data the propagation softens
the apparent kurtosis roughly four-fold
(\(\mathrm{kurt}_{\mathrm{exc}}: 6.1 \to 2.4\)).\todo[inline]{\(\DA\),
\(\DB\), \(\nuhat\), and the kurtosis values were measured under
the predecessor Gaussian-kernel parameterisation; see the umbrella
note at Section~\ref{sec:limits}.}

\paragraph{The one-step conditional law remains heavy-tailed.} The
propagation softening is a property of the multi-step forecast
object, not of the underlying conditional law: the per-anchor
library residual has \(\mathrm{kurt}_{\mathrm{exc}} \approx
24\)--\(33\) (one-step, local, no propagation), and that figure is
preprocessing-independent. Section~\ref{sec:qualifier-verdict}
quantifies this one-step departure directly and examines what can be
done about it.%
\footnote{Kurtosis range measured under the predecessor
Gaussian-kernel parameterisation; the structural claim (the scalar
coord-0 innovation is heavy-tailed) is parameterisation-independent.}

\section{Application and results}
\label{sec:application}

\subsection{Ontario pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

The instantiation is a four-stage pipeline on IESO public
\texttt{PUB\_Demand}: hourly demand is de-seasonalised to a stationary
\(z\)-score (Section~\ref{sec:zscore}); each hour is tagged
\(\{\mathrm{weekday}, \mathrm{saturday}, \mathrm{sunday}\}\) on a
single configured day-anchor (matching IESO's HE1--HE24 delivery-day
convention); an embedding dimension is selected per day-type by an
elbow rule on the prediction-skill-vs-dimension curve; and the
Section~\ref{sec:method} estimator is fit per anchor. The delivery-day
window and the day-type clock share a single source of truth, so
multi-step trajectories do not cross an un-modelled day-type
seam.\footnote{Three calendar days are excluded at load time as
non-representative of normal demand dynamics: 14--16 August 2003, the
Northeast blackout and its grid-restoration window.}

\subsection{The \(z\)-score transform is a modelling choice, not a
preprocessing detail}
\label{sec:zscore}

De-seasonalisation is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:zscore}
z \;=\; \frac{D - \mu_{m,h}}{\sigma_{m,h}},
\end{equation}
with \((\mu_{m,h}, \sigma_{m,h})\) a month \(\times\) hour-of-day
climatology estimated strictly from pre-cutoff data. This one
climatology object enters all four estimator layers --- the embedded
state, the drift fit, the diffusion (\(\Sj\) is the covariance of
\(z\)-residuals), and the demand-space round-trip --- so it is an
implicit seasonal model coupled into \(\kQ\), not a benign
preprocessing step, and two questions about its specification were
investigated on the development-set window.

The phase-asymmetry structure of Section~\ref{sec:caveat} is not a
de-seasonalisation artefact: in raw-demand units, the phase
correlation \(\mathrm{corr}(\dd D/\dd t,\, e)\) is \(-0.247\),
\(-0.250\), \(-0.217\) under the global, month\,\(\times\)\,hour, and
no-transform specifications --- near-identical, and already present in
raw demand. The transform does, however, earn its place on accuracy:
re-fitting the pipeline end-to-end gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:zscore-mape}
\MAPE \;=\; 3.86\%\ \text{(full)}
\;\to\; 11.98\%\ \text{(scale-only, }z = D/\sigma_{m,h})
\;\to\; 13.57\%\ \text{(raw demand)}.
\end{equation}
The interval-coverage breakdown locates the mechanism: the raw-demand
pipeline delivers uniform-width intervals that over-cover overnight
and under-cover at the midday peak --- the signature of the missing
\(\sigma_{m,h}\) scale step. Both sub-steps are load-bearing; the
month\,\(\times\)\,hour climatology is retained.

\subsection{Levers against the heavy-tailed conditional law}
\label{sec:qualifier-verdict}

Given the heavy-tailed one-step innovation of
Section~\ref{sec:caveat}, the \(\kQ\) construction admits four design
changes within the lagged-\(z\) embedding: a Student-\(t\) local
kernel; state augmentation; per-horizon direct fitting; and the
combined state-augmentation-plus-kernel variant. Each was tested on
the development set against an independent measurement of how much
non-Gaussian mass is present.

A Patra--Sen consistent two-component-mixture
estimator~\cite{PatraSen2016}, with the predicted-Gaussian as the
known component, applied to pooled standardised per-anchor library
residuals from the production estimation path, places
\(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} \in [0.49,\, 0.74]\) of the per-anchor library
mass in the non-Gaussian component across day-types and
preprocessings --- a conservative lower bound by their Lemma~4.
Against \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%} = 0.002\) for a pipeline-aware
Gaussian-innovation null, and \(0.26\) the maximum any single
heavy-tailed AR(1) reproduces, the contamination is
mixture-structured, not a small heavy-tail perturbation, and
preprocessing-independent.

Yet on the development set (\(n = 132\) days) only the Student-\(t\)
kernel delivers a clean log-loss gain on routine-day data
(\(\approx 1.8\%\) at mid horizons, CI excludes zero); state
augmentation, per-horizon fitting, and the combined variant are null.
The gain is far below the contamination mass just measured. The
consistent reading is that the contamination is structurally
orthogonal to the lagged-\(z\) state on which all four levers
operate: reducing it requires exogenous information --- temperature,
weather regime, calendar event flags --- not further variation within
the past-demand information set.%
\footnote{Patra--Sen \(\hat\alpha_L^{95\%}\) range and the
Student-\(t\)-kernel \(\approx 1.8\%\) gain were measured under the
predecessor Gaussian-kernel parameterisation; see the umbrella note
at Section~\ref{sec:limits}.}

\subsection{Out-of-sample backtest}
\label{sec:backtest}

The predictor is backtested on Ontario ground truth for
\(|\mathcal{D}| = 500\) complete delivery days strictly after the
model-specification cutoff; fits and \(z\)-score climatology use only
\(\le\)-cutoff data, and post-cutoff actuals enter only as query
history and scoring truth. With forecast \(\hat{y}_{d,h}\) and actual
\(y_{d,h}\) over delivery days \(d \in \mathcal{D}\) and horizons
\(h \in \{1, \ldots, 24\}\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mae-mape}
\MAE \;=\; \frac{1}{24|\mathcal{D}|}
\sum_{d \in \mathcal{D}} \sum_{h=1}^{24}
\bigl| \hat{y}_{d,h} - y_{d,h} \bigr|,
\qquad
\MAPE \;=\; \frac{1}{24|\mathcal{D}|}
\sum_{d \in \mathcal{D}} \sum_{h=1}^{24}
\frac{\bigl| \hat{y}_{d,h} - y_{d,h} \bigr|}{y_{d,h}} \,.
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:multistep} gives two propagation modes (mean
iteration and SMC); each is run under two bandwidth choices ---
production state-dependent \(\theta(x)\) via Eq.~\ref{eq:simplextheta}
and global-OLS \(\theta = 0\) --- and SMC under both Gaussian
(Eq.~\ref{eq:smc-step}) and empirical-residual innovation sampling.
The full \(\{\text{propagation}\} \times \{\theta\} \times
\{\text{innovation law}\}\) grid is six cells, labelled
\(\mathrm{A}\)--\(\mathrm{F}\). Point error follows a diurnal
envelope (Figure~\ref{fig:diurnal}) --- a property of the demand
process under a day-anchored delivery-day clock, not of any
predictor --- with the lowest values at deep night and the peak
near \(h \approx 11\) (HE\,11). The cell-to-cell separation, by
contrast, is small: the grid's lowest \(\MAE\) is cell B
(mean-iteration, \(\theta = 0\), \(751.6\)~MW), the highest is
cell A (\(768.8\)~MW), and all six lie within a \(17\)-MW band
over the full \(500\)-day population. The
production-\(\theta\) cells (A, E) are uniformly worse than the
\(\theta = 0\) counterparts (B, C, D, F) by a few to a few-dozen
MW at most horizons (Figure~\ref{fig:per-horizon}); under
multi-step iteration the simplex-interpolated \(\theta(x)\) field
costs MAE rather than buying it. Point accuracy is not the
discriminating axis. Interval coverage is
(Section~\ref{sec:distributional}): the Gaussian band of the
mean-iteration cells covers \(\approx 21\%\) of actuals against a
nominal \(\approx 68.3\%\) one-sigma band --- the under-coverage
signature of a Gaussian interval around a heavy-tailed innovation
--- and SMC restores it to \(68\)--\(72\%\).

\subsection{Distributional diagnostics on the SMC predictive law}
\label{sec:distributional}

The full ensemble at each \((d, h)\) of the SMC cells C--F of
Section~\ref{sec:backtest} carries information the point/interval
summary does not surface: how the predictive distribution is shaped,
where in the level set the band misses, and whether the per-step
innovation pushed forward by Eq.~\ref{eq:smc-step} reaches the actual
in the tails. Three diagnostics are reported.

\emph{Multi-sigma coverage} (Figure~\ref{fig:multisigma}). For
nominal central levels
\(\alpha \in \{0.50, 0.683, 0.80, 0.90, 0.95, 0.99\}\), the
empirical coverage of the corresponding sample-quantile band reveals
which cells calibrate across the full range and which do not.
Empirical-residual cells D and F ride the nominal diagonal within
\(\approx 1\) percentage point at every level; Gaussian-SMC cells C
and E bulge above the diagonal at small levels and dip below it at
large levels, the signature of a Gaussian tail mildly
under-dispersed against a heavier-tailed truth.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_multisigma_coverage.pdf}
\caption{Empirical vs.\ nominal coverage at six central levels for
the four SMC cells, pooled over horizons and day-types
(\(|\mathcal{D}| = 500\)). Diagonal is perfect calibration. The
empirical-residual cells (D, F) calibrate at every level; the
Gaussian-SMC cells (C, E) are mildly under-dispersed in the

predictive distribution.}
\label{fig:multisigma}
\end{figure}

\emph{PIT shape} (Figure~\ref{fig:pit}). The probability-integral
transform of the actual through the per-step predictive distribution
exposes the residual mis-calibration the coverage diagonal smooths
over. All four SMC cells exhibit a systematic right-decreasing
density: actuals fall in the lower half of the predictive more often
than uniform expectation, indicating a small forecast-high bias
shared across propagation modes and innovation laws. The bias is a
property of the conditional-mean factor \(\Cj\) and is not
the SMC sampler's to repair.

\emph{Ensemble shape and CRPS.} At \(h = 1\) the per-anchor sample
excess-kurtosis averaged across the population is
\(\overline{\mathrm{kurt}_{\mathrm{exc}}} \approx +4.8\) for the
empirical-residual cells D and F and \(\approx -0.04\) for the
Gaussian cells C and E: the empirical sampler injects the heavy tail
measured at the library-residual layer (Section~\ref{sec:caveat},
one-step \(\mathrm{kurt}_{\mathrm{exc}} \approx 24\)--\(33\)) into
the predictive distribution; the Gaussian sampler does not. The
sample-form CRPS (with the \(M/(M-1)\) unbiased correction), pooled
over all \((d, h, \mathrm{daytype})\), ranks
\(C\, (539.2) < D\, (542.2) < E\, (547.6) < F\, (550.7)\)~MW: the
Gaussian-SMC cells score better on the proper rule (sharpness wins
on average); the empirical-SMC cells match nominal coverage at
every level. The classical sharpness-versus-calibration trade-off,
made visible by the diagnostic.

\paragraph{Liu--Gao pre-registration.} The six-cell grid of
Section~\ref{sec:backtest} was pre-registered as a test of
contamination-pattern predictions adapted
from~\cite{LiuGao2019} for local-Gaussian forecasters under
heavy-tailed innovations: a
Gaussian-SMC step under-covers (P1), an empirical-residual step
restores nominal coverage (P2), the gap between mean-iteration and
SMC coverage is large (P4), and the empirical sampler is less
\(\theta\)-sensitive than the Gaussian sampler (P3). Pooled across
the population P4 is confirmed (\(\approx 50\) percentage-point
coverage gain for SMC over mean iteration at the one-sigma band).
Day-type-stratified, P4 is weekday-specific (\(+52\)-percentage-point
gain to \(75\)--\(78\%\) coverage); on weekends the empirical-SMC
cells under-cover the one-sigma band by \(12\)--\(19\) points,
falsifying P2 for the sunday cell with statistical force under a
day-bootstrap test. The sunday d-vs-day-type confound is not resolved
within this paper.

\subsection{Registered forward prediction experiment}
\label{sec:registered}

A fair retrospective IESO comparison is not recoverable
(Section~\ref{sec:ieso}), so the comparison is run forward as a
pre-registered experiment. The full model specification --- estimator
identity, resolved hyperparameters, data cutoff \(t_{\mathrm{cut}}\),
code commit, and a content hash of the \(\le t_{\mathrm{cut}}\)
actuals --- is committed before any forecast is issued; because the
predictor is lazy (per-anchor fit at prediction time), that
specification pins both the function and its inputs. Forecasts then
issue forward against settled actuals, scored on persistence
baselines with IESO published values recorded as differently-horizoned
context. The enforcement detail --- content-hash recheck at load, the
post-\(t_{\mathrm{cut}}\) cutoff guard, the append-only revision
ledger --- is given in Appendix~\ref{app:estimator}.

\subsection{The retrospective IESO head-to-head is infeasible from
public archives}
\label{sec:ieso}

Can the forecast be compared retrospectively against IESO's own
published forecast on historical settled days? A fair comparison
requires two properties of an IESO archive: (i) the values reported
must be on the Ontario-demand basis (matching the forecast target
of this paper), and (ii) the values reported must be the day-ahead
issue (matching its horizon). The simpler hypothesis is that some
public IESO archive supplies both. To test it, all 133 public IESO
report directories were enumerated and each checked against the two
properties.

The simpler hypothesis fails, via two independent blockers. The
Ontario-demand-basis archive (Adequacy3
\texttt{ForecastOntDemand}) retains, for days old enough to have
settled actuals, only the end-of-delivery-day version --- so its
horizon is wrong (property (ii) violated). The day-ahead-issued
archives (DATotals, PredispTotals) publish only market totals --- so
their basis is wrong (property (i) violated): ``Total Load'' carries
a definitional \(\approx +2485~\mathrm{MW}\) offset over Ontario
Demand (MarketQuantity enumeration: Total
Energy/Loss/Load/Dispatchable/10S/10N/30R; none is Ontario demand).
No archive satisfies both. The retrospective comparison is
infeasible from public data; hence the registered forward design
of Section~\ref{sec:registered}. (Stated for public archives as of
the recorded access date.)

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}
\label{sec:limits}

\todo[inline]{The specific empirical values cited below
(\(\approx 4.8\%\), \(\approx 2.3\%\), the \(4\times\) kurtosis
softening, the \(1.8\%\) Student-\(t\)-kernel gain, the \(49\)--\(74\%\)
Patra--Sen range) are the development-set decomposition figures of
Section~\ref{sec:caveat-decomposition} and
Section~\ref{sec:qualifier-verdict}. They were measured under the
predecessor Gaussian-kernel parameterisation; the S-map kernel
(Eq.~\ref{eq:weights}) was a re-parameterisation of the same
local-linear-with-Gaussian-residual fit and is expected to leave the
qualitative structure (mean-bias and shape-departure contributions,
contamination orthogonal to the lagged-\(z\) state) unchanged.}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Estimator limitations (Section~\ref{sec:caveat}).}
  \(\Pij\) departs from \(\kQ\) in location (\(\Cj\)
  phase-mis-centred) and shape (\(\Sj\) Gaussian proxy of a
  heavy-tailed innovation). The decomposition bounds the recoverable
  loss at \(\approx 4.8\%\) and \(\approx 2.3\%\) of development-set
  log-loss --- small in both directions, with the heavy-tail interval
  including zero. Multi-step propagation softens the apparent
  kurtosis \(\approx 4\times\); the one-step conditional law remains
  heavy-tailed at the library-residual level.

  \item \textbf{Within-\(z\) levers (Section~\ref{sec:qualifier-verdict}).}
  Of the four candidate design changes within the lagged-\(z\)
  embedding --- a Student-\(t\) kernel, state augmentation,
  per-horizon direct fitting, and the combined variant --- only the
  Student-\(t\) kernel delivers a clean log-loss improvement, and
  the effect is modest (\(\approx 1.8\%\) at mid horizons). State
  augmentation and the combined variant are null.

  \item \textbf{Scope of extraction.} The data-measure semigroup
  \(\{\Pi_t\}\) exists by Chapman--Kolmogorov on the
  temporally-coherent measure (Section~\ref{sec:construction}
  qualifier 3); what is extracted is the single-step slice
  \(\widehat{\Pidelta}\), and Section~\ref{sec:multistep} iterates
  this slice. The CK consistency gate (Table~\ref{tab:gates}) confirms
  on a VAR(1) ground truth that the iterate \((\widehat{\Pidelta})^h\)
  matches the directly-extractable \(\widehat{\Pi}_{h\Delta}\) within
  the gate's tolerance at \(h \le 24\); an analogous inspection-only
  probe run in-sample on the Ontario embedding finds the gap
  diverging fast with \(h\) (Figure~\ref{fig:ck-divergence}).
  Quantitatively, the relative-Frobenius diffusion error reaches
  \(\approx 50\%\) at \(h = 24\) across day-types --- a thirty-fold
  inflation against the synthetic VAR(1) baseline. The data-measure
  semigroup exists; the iterated estimator does not approximate it
  at long horizons. The headroom a direct multi-horizon estimator
  \(\{\widehat{\Pi}_{h\Delta}\}_{h \in H}\) --- fit by the same WLS
  construction on the \((x_t, x_{t+h})\) library at each \(h\) ---
  can recover is bounded above by precisely this inconsistency.
  A jointly CK-constrained estimator is a further escalation,
  distinct from this paper.

  \item \textbf{Exogenous information.} No weather/exogenous
  covariates. The Patra--Sen quantification places at least
  \(49\)--\(74\%\) of per-anchor library mass in a non-Gaussian
  mixture component that the within-\(z\) levers do not access;
  reducing the heavy-tailed departure requires exogenous information
  rather than further variation within the \(z\) information set.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Recorded results carry two content
hashes: an inputs hash (git commit, model-specification hash, library
versions, RNG seeds, declared inputs) and a body hash recomputed at
read time. The recording refuses to write from a dirty source tree.
Exploratory development-set material is marked as such and is not
cited as a result elsewhere in the paper.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The two ways \(\Pij\) departs from \(\kQ\) --- a phase-mis-centred
conditional mean and a Gaussian-proxy diffusion --- were each
quantified by an oracle decomposition, and the recoverable loss is
small in both directions on the full delivery-day population. Of the
four design changes available within the lagged-\(z\) embedding, only
the Student-\(t\) kernel delivers a clean log-loss improvement, and
it is modest (\(\approx 1.8\%\) at mid horizons). A Patra--Sen
contamination estimate places at least \(49\)--\(74\%\) of the
per-anchor library mass in a non-Gaussian mixture component ---
substantial mass the within-\(z\) levers do not reach. The consistent
reading is that the contamination is structurally orthogonal to the
lagged-\(z\) state: reducing the heavy-tailed departure of the
conditional law requires \emph{exogenous information} (temperature,
weather regime, calendar event flags), not further variation within
the past-demand information set. The estimator's evaluation is run
forward as a registered prediction experiment, the retrospective
IESO head-to-head being infeasible from public archives.%
\footnote{The cited \(1.8\%\) and \(49\)--\(74\%\) values are the
Section~\ref{sec:qualifier-verdict} measurements under the
predecessor Gaussian-kernel parameterisation; see the umbrella note
at Section~\ref{sec:limits}.}

\section{Estimator detail}
\label{app:estimator}

The drift \(\Cj\) (Eq.~\ref{eq:wls}) is computed as the least-squares
solution of the row-scaled system
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rowscale}
\sqrt{W}\,X\,C \;=\; \sqrt{W}\,Y,
\qquad W = \operatorname{diag}(w_i),\ w_i \ge 0,
\end{equation}
not by forming the normal equations \((X^{\top}WX)^{-1}X^{\top}WY\):
the two coincide, and the row-scaled solve via a pseudoinverse
degrades gracefully when the scaled design is rank-deficient (a query
state with few non-negligible weights) rather than failing. The
diffusion \(\Sj\) (Eq.~\ref{eq:diff}) is the plain, unweighted
covariance of the residuals \(r_i = y_i - x_i\Cj\); \(W\) localises
which pairs enter the fit, but the second moment is taken without
further reweighting, for the collapse reason of
Section~\ref{sec:construction}. The anchor's own pair is removed from
\(\mathcal{L}\) before the fit, so \(\Cj\) and \(\Sj\) are
leave-one-out at the anchor.

The bandwidth \(\theta^{*}\) minimises the leave-one-out objective
\(\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{LOO}}\) (Eq.~\ref{eq:loocv}) over a fixed
dimensionless grid
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:thetagrid}
\{\theta_k\}_{k=1}^{K}
\;=\; \operatorname{linspace}(0, \theta_{\max}; K),
\end{equation}
spanning the full local--global range: \(\theta = 0\) is the global
linear fit (uniform weights), and \(\theta_{\max}\) is the tightest
locality the grid resolves. True leave-one-out refits \(\Cj^{(-i)}\)
for every held-out row, at cost \(O(m^{2})\) per grid point; it is
evaluated exactly on a fixed random subsample of \(150\) library
pairs (the full library when \(|\mathcal{L}| \le 150\)), drawn from a
seeded generator for reproducibility.\footnote{The naive
refit-per-row form is used in place of a hat-matrix shortcut, which
was found to be both buggy and sensitive to the weighting convention;
the subsample evaluation is exact and costs \(\approx 0.1\,\)s per
call.} The fitting stage consumes the grid argmin directly --- no
rail-pin guard, edge fallback, or clipping. Because the S-map kernel
(Eq.~\ref{eq:weights}) carries no \(\theta\)-dependent normaliser,
the LOO objective has a well-posed left endpoint at \(\theta = 0\)
and admits the global linear operator as a finite, achievable
selection rather than an unattained limit.

Estimation at an anchor and the multi-step forecast generator of
Section~\ref{sec:caveat} call one local-fit routine, which maps a
library \((X, Y)\) and an explicit query state to
\((\Cj, \Sj, \bar r, \theta^{*}, r)\); estimation derives the query
from an embedding and an anchor, the forecast generator supplies a
post-cutoff query against a pre-cutoff library. The forecast path is
therefore the estimation path --- the backtest numbers of
Section~\ref{sec:backtest} come from the code the synthetic recovery
gate of Section~\ref{sec:synth} validates, not a re-implementation.

The forward experiment of Section~\ref{sec:registered} enforces its
committed specification mechanically. The content hash of the
\(\le t_{\mathrm{cut}}\) actuals recorded at registration is
recomputed at every prediction-time load and compared against the
stored value; any divergence raises rather than scores. A cutoff
guard rejects any row dated after \(t_{\mathrm{cut}}\) from entering
a fit. Settled actuals accumulate in an append-only ledger,
accommodating IESO's published-demand revisions as new appended
records rather than overwrites, and the ledger state is itself
hashed into each score.
